## The Central Truth

**Personality is not a costume you wear for approval.**  
The human personality is expressed through four attributes of personal development: **Behavioral, Temperamental, Emotional, and Mental**.

UDHR Article 26:2 directs education toward the **full development of the human personality**. Education does not manufacture a teleios person. The person is teleios by design. Education makes the person mindful of that design, teaches its architecture, and equips the person to espouse who they are and live accordingly.

Do not reverse the ontology: you do not practice the attributes and thereby become a person, just as a car does not drive and thereby become a car. Your practices are responsibility, accountability, and life flowing from what you are.

## Core Teaching

### 1) UDHR 26:2 — the public grammar of formation

Article 26:2 (SBD applied reading): education shall be directed to the **full development of the human personality** and to strengthening respect for human rights and freedoms.

SBD’s claim is not “the UN invents anthropology.” The claim is:

- public human-rights language recognizes the **full development of the human personality** as an educational mandate  
- education concerns the whole person, not information alone  
- formation means becoming mindful of design, learning its architecture, espousing it, and living accordingly  
- “just be yourself” is empty when “self” has been detached from design  
- authenticity without accountability is often refusal of design with a flattering name  

School 1 Course 02 installs this as anthropology. Later courses deepen each domain.

**Maturity is not produced by the educational activity.** Maturity is the state of being mature — full development. Education enables responsible consciousness and expression of that state.

### 2) The Four Attributes (personality domains)

Canon §V.2 lists **Four Attributes:** Behavioral, Temperamental, Emotional, Mental.

| Attribute | Definition from the PD map | Responsibility question | Refusal / distortion |
|-----------|----------------------------|-------------------------|----------------------|
| **Behavioral** | Self-control through action and reaction: self-command, self-possession, self-will / will power | Does my action and reaction express self-possession? | Impulse, inconsistency, outsourced responsibility, domination |
| **Temperamental** | Disposition: natural disposition, characteristic tendency, nature-state | Do I understand and steward my disposition without using it as an excuse? | “That’s just who I am,” volatility, rigidity, personality performance |
| **Emotional** | Development of affective states: valence, arousal, and motivational intensity | Can I recognize affective state and remain accountable for what I do with it? | Emotional tyranny, numbness, manipulation, denial |
| **Mental** | Development of the mind (faculty of reason) and intellectual processes | Am I mindful, educated, and reasoning from design? | Fantasy, rationalization, hearsay, refusal to think |

**Critical distinction (do not blur):**

| | **Attributes (this course)** | **Faculties (Course 04)** |
|--|------------------------------|---------------------------|
| What | Attributes of personal development | Capacities / powers of the person |
| List | Behavioral, Temperamental, Emotional, Mental | Conative, Cognitive, Psychomotor, Affective |
| PD-map function | Full development of human personality | Commitment/striving action; learning/reasoning; physical/mental agility; care/forethought/empathy |

Attributes are not faculties with different names. Course 04 will address the capacities directly. **Week 3 is attributes only.**

### 3) Why marriage exposes attributes

Marriage reveals whether a person espouses the attributes and lives accordingly:

- unfinished behavioral patterns  
- unexamined temperament  
- ungoverned emotion  
- lazy or deceptive thinking  

Partners often misdiagnose:

| Misdiagnosis | Real attribute issue |
|--------------|----------------------|
| “We don’t communicate” | Mental and emotional attributes may be misunderstood or irresponsibly lived |
| “We’re incompatible” | Temperament may be used as an excuse while behavior refuses accountability |
| “The spark died” | Emotional and behavioral responsibility may have been neglected |
| “They’re toxic” | Sometimes true — but the label can also hide two persons refusing attribute stewardship |
| “I married the wrong person” | Sometimes true — but it can also conceal refusal to espouse one’s own design |

Anthropology of Wholeness insists: **understand the attribute before turning the symptom into an identity or verdict.**

### 4) Formation order inside Week 3

Do not try to “fix everything.” Use this educational order:

1. **Learn** the definition of all four attributes  
2. **Audit** where you misunderstand or refuse each one  
3. **Choose two** requiring greater mindfulness and accountability  
4. **Live accordingly** for 7 days and document the evidence  
5. **Review** on Day 7 and carry the responsibility into Week 4  

The seven-day practice does not make you teleios. It exposes whether your life agrees with the teleios person you are by design.

### 5) Attribute portraits (teachable depth)

**Behavioral.**  
The attribute of self-control through action and reaction: possession, self-command, self-possession, self-will / will power. Kept promises and congruent conduct are fruits, not its ontology.

**Temperamental.**  
Disposition — natural disposition, characteristic tendency, nature-state. Temperament is real; it is neither a sin nor a permanent exemption. Responsibility means knowing and stewarding it without inventing a costume.

**Emotional.**  
The development of affective states: valence, arousal, and motivational intensity. Emotion is not identity and not law. Responsibility begins with literacy about the state and accountability for its expression.

**Mental.**  
The development of the mind — the faculty of reason — and intellectual processes. Mental responsibility includes thinking, examining premises, distinguishing design facts from preference, and refusing hearsay as anthropology.

## Case Contrast

### Emotional high / Behavioral low
Beautiful feelings, unreliable life. Covenant partners experience them as abandonment with poetry. Songs without structure.

### Mental high / Emotional low
Brilliant analysis, cold house. They win arguments and lose intimacy. Correct and lonely.

### Temperamental idolatry
“I’m just intense / chill / direct.” Translation: I refuse responsibility for how my disposition is lived. The home must orbit my default.

### Behavioral polish / Mental fantasy
Looks disciplined; thinks in romance novels and catastrophe scripts. The calendar is ordered; the inner story is not.

The teleios personality is **whole by design**. Integration is the responsible expression of all four attributes — not one polished attribute used to conceal refusal in the others.

## Common Errors This Week

1. **Calling attributes “faculties”** — blurs Course 04. Keep the vocabulary clean.  
2. **Treating the audit as a measurement of human worth or completeness**  
3. **Thinking seven days of drills manufacture maturity** — the car does not become a car by driving  
4. **Using audit scores to shame a spouse** — this audit is first for *you*.  
5. **Confusing temperament with sin** — steward the style; repent the harm.

## Daily Drills (7 days)

Choose practices for the two attributes requiring greater mindfulness/accountability. These practices educate and expose responsible living; they do not manufacture teleios.

### Behavioral drill (10 min)
Pick one kept promise today (message, chore, apology, payment, workout). Log: *promised → done → time*. Add one delayed impulse: *urge → wait → chosen act*.

### Temperamental drill (10 min)
Name your default (e.g., fast/slow, loud/quiet, conflict-approach/avoid). Practice the **opposite skill** once today for someone else’s good. Log it. Ask: *Did love require stretch?*

### Emotional drill (10 min)
When emotion rises: name it → breathe → choose one non-reactive act. Log: *trigger → feeling → chosen act → repair if needed*.

### Mental drill (10 min)
Take one conflict story. Split into three columns: Facts | Interpretations | Desired truth-seeking question. Ask the question of someone safe (or journal the honest answer). Cross out one fantasy sentence.

### Day 7
Review all four. Note what you learned, where you espoused design, and where you refused it. Write one sentence per attribute: *What I now understand and how I am responsible to live accordingly*.
