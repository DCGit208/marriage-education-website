CONVERSATION FRAMEWORK GUIDE

The step-by-step LVWI script for empathy-first marriage literacy advocacy

L Listen 80% of time
V Validate Empathy first
W Witness Your story
I Invite Clear next step
F Follow Up Stay connected
STEP
1

LISTEN — Give them the floor (80% of the conversation)

Your goal: understand their need before you open any door.

  • Ask open-ended questions — never yes/no
  • Let them talk without interrupting; resist the urge to fix
  • Identify their psychological state: Protection, Relief, or Hope?
  • Listen for the root emotion beneath the surface complaint
Example phrases

"Tell me more about that."  ·  "What's that like for you?"  ·  "How long has this been going on?"  ·  "What have you tried so far?"

STEP
2

VALIDATE — Name the emotion, honor the struggle

Your goal: make them feel heard before they can be helped.

  • Name what they feel — don't minimize, don't compare
  • Never move to solutions until validation is genuinely felt
  • You can validate without agreeing with their conclusions
Example phrases

"That sounds really hard."  ·  "I hear you."  ·  "That makes complete sense given what you've been through."  ·  "I've been there too."

STEP
3

WITNESS — Share your own experience with the resource

Your goal: demonstrate change, not credentials.

  • Be specific: "Before I read SBD, I…" / "After, I…"
  • Stay humble: "I'm still learning, but this helped me"
  • Be vulnerable — not preachy, not a lecture
  • Your testimony removes the skepticism no pitch ever could
Example phrases

"I was in a similar place. What changed for me was..."  ·  "I read this book and honestly — I wasn't expecting much. But it shifted something in how I..."

STEP
4

INVITE — Offer one clear, pressure-free next step

Your goal: open a door, not push someone through it.

  • Give exactly ONE specific invitation — not a menu of options
  • Speak to their mode: financial concern? Offer sponsored access
  • Frame it as an invitation, never a pitch or obligation
  • If they say no — respect it and stay connected anyway
Example phrases

"Would you like me to send you the link?"  ·  "Can I gift you this book?"  ·  "What if we read it together and discuss it over coffee in two weeks?"

STEP
5

FOLLOW UP — Stay in the journey with them

Your goal: advocate through completion, not just at enrollment.

  • Set a specific follow-up date before the conversation ends
  • Track progress on your First 10 Beneficiaries worksheet
  • Ask open-ended follow-up questions — don't quiz, accompany
  • Celebrate every milestone, no matter how small
Example phrases

"I'll check in next week to see how it's going."  ·  "Let me know if anything raises a question — I'm here."  ·  "Let's plan to talk again in two weeks."

Full Practice Script — Scenario: Communication Breakdown

Setting: A friend mentions they are struggling in their marriage

YOU
"Tell me more — what's the hardest part right now?" ← Listen
FRIEND
"We just can't communicate. Every conversation turns into a fight."
YOU
"That sounds exhausting. I've been there. We used to fight over everything — and I didn't even know why." ← Validate + Witness
YOU
"I read a book called Communication Fidelity and it completely changed how my spouse and I talk. It's practical — not theory. Would you be open to checking it out?" ← Witness + Invite
FRIEND
"Maybe. How much is it?"
YOU
"1,500 FCFA — less than a meal out. And honestly, if money is tight right now, I'd be happy to gift it to you. This is too important to let budget stop you." ← Access-focus
FRIEND
"Okay, let's try it."
YOU
"Great. I'll send you the link tonight. Let's plan to have coffee in two weeks and you can tell me what stood out. Deal?" ← Follow Up

3-Minute Diagnostic — Identifying Psychological Mode

Ask This If They Say… Mode Likely Is Your Next Move
"How are things in your relationship right now?" "Fine" or "We're okay" (dismissive) Protection Slow down. Validate. Don't pitch. Offer free resource.
"How are things in your relationship right now?" "Honestly, it's been hard lately…" Relief Match resource to stated pain. Give clear next step.
"How are things in your relationship right now?" "Good — we just want to go deeper together" Hope Cast vision. Invite to Foundation School or advanced resource.
"What's the hardest part right now?" "I don't know" or silence Protection Share your story. Make it safe. Exit gracefully if needed.
"Have you tried anything to address it?" "We've tried everything — nothing works" Protection Empathize first. Don't promise your resource will work. Just share your experience.
"Have you tried anything to address it?" "I know I need better tools" Relief You're in — match resource directly to their pain point.

The 4 Levels of Listening

1 Waiting to Talk

You're physically present but mentally preparing your response. The other person can feel it.

2 Surface Listening

You hear words but miss tone, emotion, and the unspoken need underneath.

3 Empathic Listening

You hear content AND emotion. You ask clarifying questions. You resist the urge to fix.

4 Soul Listening

You are fully present — body, mind, spirit. You sense what isn't said. You hold the space.

Target: Level 3 in every advocacy conversation. Reach for Level 4 in repeated, trusted relationships. Never operate at Level 1 or 2 — if you're not ready to truly listen, reschedule the conversation.

3 Deadly Advocacy Errors to Avoid

Error #1 — Wrong Timing

Pushing solutions before building trust. Pitching to someone in Protection Mode before they feel safe.
"You just need to buy this book!"

Error #2 — Wrong Match

Giving generic advice instead of specific resources. Not matching the tool to the actual pain point the person named.

Error #3 — Wrong Level

Offering basic resources to advanced learners. Telling someone in Hope Mode about the introductory book when they're ready for Foundation School.
