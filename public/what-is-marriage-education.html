1. Marriage Education: Definition & Core Philosophy

Marriage education is proactive, systematic training designed to equip individuals and couples with the knowledge, frameworks, and skills necessary to build and sustain thriving marriages according to design principles—not cultural trends.

Core Premise: Marriage is not intuitive—it's designed. Just as you can't build a house without understanding architecture, you can't build a marriage without understanding its design. Marriage education provides that blueprint.

The Marriage Literacy Crisis

Consider this paradox:

  • We require thousands of hours of training to become doctors, lawyers, or engineers
  • We require driver's licenses to operate vehicles
  • We require certifications to practice most professions
  • But we expect marriages to thrive based on feelings alone

The result? 50% divorce rate. Not because marriage is broken, but because couples are marriage-illiterate.

What Makes Our Approach Different

Marriage Education anchors in design principles, not cultural myths:

Marriage is about happiness Marriage is about faithfulness

Good marriages are easy Good marriages are practiced

2. Marriage Education vs. Marriage Therapy

Marriage education and marriage therapy serve different purposes:

Marriage Therapy (Remedial)

  • When: After problems arise
  • Focus: Healing wounds, resolving conflicts, addressing pathology
  • Approach: Clinical, diagnostic, problem-focused
  • Duration: Often ongoing, no fixed end

Marriage Education (Proactive)

  • When: Before marriage or to strengthen existing marriages
  • Focus: Building frameworks, developing skills, establishing disciplines
  • Approach: Educational, systematic, growth-focused
  • Duration: Structured programs with clear milestones

Both have value. But here's the key: education prevents most problems therapy tries to fix.

Think of it like physical health:

  • Therapy = Surgery after illness
  • Education = Nutrition, exercise, and preventive care

3. The Sex By Design (SBD) Framework

Our marriage education methodology is built on the Sex By Design (SBD) framework—a comprehensive system that treats marriage as a sexual civilization, not a polite roommate contract.

Core Principles of Sex By Design

1. Marriage is designed, not evolved
We don't make up marriage rules based on feelings. We submit to the design revealed in scripture, law, and human anthropology.

2. Sex = Marriage (One-Flesh Reality)
Sex isn't something you do after marriage; it is marriage. The covenant is enacted, renewed, and expressed through one-flesh union.

3. Sexuality is a lifestyle, not just "sex acts"
Sex is the product. Sexuality is the lifestyle: nakedness, allure, seduction, conjugal imagination—all ordered disciplines.

4. Teleios formation before (or alongside) marriage
We don't use marriage to fix broken people. Personal wholeness (behavioral, emotional, temperamental, mental maturity) is taught as your human responsibility.

5. Practice from perfection, not toward it
Your identity determines your behavior. You don't "try to become" a faithful spouse—you practice what you already are in Christ.

The Three Formation Pathways

🔵 School 1: Foundation School

Teleios formation—becoming whole before (or while in) marriage. Behavioral maturity, emotional steadiness, temperamental self-awareness.

🔴 School 2: Sexual Literacy School

Sexuality as lifestyle: nakedness, seduction, conjugal imagination, Pics & Pearls, Song of Songs liturgies.

🟢 School 3: Marriage Systems School

Governance, communication, family systems, conflict resolution, financial stewardship.

4. What You Learn in Marriage Education

Marriage education covers four essential domains:

Domain 1: Personal Formation (Teleios)

  • Behavioral maturity: Self-control, responsibility, discipline
  • Emotional steadiness: Responding vs. reacting
  • Temperamental awareness: Understanding your design
  • Mental clarity: Sound thinking and wisdom

Domain 2: Sexual Literacy

  • Understanding sex as covenant enactment (not performance)
  • Nakedness stewardship (without shame)
  • Seduction as practiced art within marriage
  • Allure, presence, and conjugal imagination
  • Pics & Pearls: visual and imaginative disciplines

Domain 3: Relational Systems

  • Communication frameworks (Green/Yellow/Red)
  • Conflict resolution protocols
  • Financial governance and transparency
  • Family systems and boundary setting
  • Parenting philosophy alignment

Domain 4: Covenant Theology

  • Marriage as covenant vs. contract
  • One-flesh reality (not metaphor)
  • Legal and scriptural foundations (UDHR 16 & 26, Genesis 1-2)
  • Kingdom purpose and legacy building

Pastors, counselors, HR professionals, and community leaders benefit from marriage education to serve others effectively.
