## Course Philosophy

**Not another "purity" talk. Not another "wait until marriage" lecture.**

This is **sexual literacy from Scripture** — understanding what sex actually *is*, why it exists, how it functions, and what it means within God's covenantal design.

**Structure Approach:**
- ✅ **One Chapter Per Week** — Exhaustive coverage of the entire book
- ✅ **Weekly Content** — Chapter summary, key concepts, discussion questions, assignments
- ✅ **Progressive Build** — Each week builds on the previous
- ✅ **Application-Heavy** — Not theory; practical implementation
- ✅ **Exam Per Chapter** — 10 questions to test comprehension

## 12-Week Course Structure

### **Week 1: Introduction — The Crisis of Sexual Illiteracy**
**Book Chapter:** Introduction  
**Core Question:** Why do Christians who "saved themselves" still struggle sexually in marriage?

**Key Concepts:**
- Sexual illiteracy epidemic (60-70% of Christian marriages report sexual dysfunction)
- "Purity culture" success at celibacy, failure at sexual literacy
- Sex is not just "something you do" — it's a **language, liturgy, covenant act**
- The difference between virginity and sexual competence

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand why "wait until marriage" is insufficient without formation
- Identify the gap between purity teaching and sexual flourishing
- Recognize sex as designed, not merely permitted

**Assignment:**
- Self-assessment: Rate your sexual literacy (1-10)
- Reflection: What did your church teach you about sex? What was missing?

---

### **Week 2: Chapter 1 — What Is Sex?**
**Core Question:** What actually happens during sex according to Genesis 2 and 1 Corinthians 6?

**Key Concepts:**
- Genesis 2:24 — "One flesh" (basar echad)
- 1 Corinthians 6:16 — Sex creates one-flesh union whether you intend it or not
- Sex is ontological (changes being), not just behavioral (physical act)
- The three meanings of sex: union, communication, creation

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Define "one flesh" biblically
- Understand sex as covenant language
- Distinguish between sex as act vs. sex as union

**Assignment:**
- Study Genesis 2:18-25 and 1 Corinthians 6:12-20
- Write: What changes in how I view sex if it creates union every time?

**Exam:** 10 questions on one-flesh theology

---

### **Week 3: Chapter 2 — The Design of Sex (Anatomy & Function)**
**Core Question:** How does the body reveal God's design for sex?

**Key Concepts:**
- Male anatomy designed for **donation** (outward, penetrating, seed-bearing)
- Female anatomy designed for **reception** (inward, receiving, life-hosting)
- Complementarity is not about roles; it's about **design architecture**
- Pleasure is not incidental; it's intentional (1 Cor 7:3-5)

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Describe male/female anatomical complementarity
- Understand pleasure as design feature, not accident
- Recognize sex as embodied theology

**Assignment:**
- Read 1 Corinthians 7:1-9
- Couples: Discuss what "due benevolence" means in your marriage
- Singles: Journal on stewardship of desire before marriage

**Exam:** 10 questions on anatomy, design, and complementarity

---

### **Week 4: Chapter 3 — The Purposes of Sex (Union, Procreation, Pleasure)**
**Core Question:** Why did God create sex? What are its purposes?

**Key Concepts:**
- **Purpose 1: Union** — "One flesh" (Gen 2:24)
- **Purpose 2: Procreation** — "Be fruitful and multiply" (Gen 1:28)
- **Purpose 3: Pleasure** — "Rejoice with the wife of thy youth" (Prov 5:18-19)
- All three purposes are valid; none cancels the others

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Identify the three biblical purposes of sex
- Defend pleasure as legitimate purpose (not just procreation)
- Understand how contraception relates to design

**Assignment:**
- Write: How does understanding sex's three purposes change how I approach it?
- Couples: Discuss which purpose you've emphasized most and which you've neglected

**Exam:** 10 questions on purposes of sex

---

### **Week 5: Chapter 4 — The Language of Sex (Communication & Intimacy)**
**Core Question:** How does sex function as a language between spouses?

**Key Concepts:**
- Sex is **non-verbal communication** — "This is my body given for you"
- Sexual frequency reveals marital health (1 Cor 7:5 — "Defraud ye not")
- Refusal communicates rejection; frequency communicates desire
- **Initiation patterns** reveal who feels wanted

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Describe sex as communication system
- Understand sexual frequency as barometer of marriage health
- Recognize initiation/refusal patterns in your marriage

**Assignment:**
- Track sexual frequency for 30 days (honest data)
- Reflection: What is your current sexual rhythm saying about your marriage?

**Exam:** 10 questions on sex as language

---

### **Week 6: Chapter 5 — The Context of Sex (Marriage as Covenant)**
**Core Question:** Why does sex belong exclusively in marriage?

**Key Concepts:**
- Sex without covenant is like **language without grammar** — chaotic
- Marriage provides the **container** for sexual expression
- Covenant = permanent, public, witnessed commitment (not private "we're committed")
- Why "engaged sex" is still fornication (no covenant yet)

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Explain why sex requires marriage (not just "love")
- Define covenant vs. contract
- Understand the protective function of marriage

**Assignment:**
- Read Hebrews 13:4; 1 Thess 4:3-8
- Write: Why is sex outside marriage described as "defrauding" your future spouse?

**Exam:** 10 questions on marriage, covenant, and sexual ethics

---

### **Week 7: Chapter 6 — The Corruption of Sex (Porneia, Adultery, Fornication)**
**Core Question:** How is sex corrupted, and what does restoration look like?

**Key Concepts:**
- **Porneia** (fornication) — sex outside covenant
- **Moicheia** (adultery) — sex that violates covenant
- Porn, masturbation, fantasy — counterfeits of covenant sex
- Redemption: Christ restores what sin corrupts

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Define biblical sexual sin categories
- Understand why porn damages covenant capacity
- Recognize the path to restoration (not just "stop sinning")

**Assignment:**
- Honest inventory: Where have I consumed counterfeits?
- Reflection: What formation practices will I install to replace counterfeits?

**Exam:** 10 questions on sexual sin and restoration

---

### **Week 8: Chapter 7 — Sexual Initiation (Roles, Desire, Pursuit)**
**Core Question:** Who initiates sex, and what does initiation communicate?

**Key Concepts:**
- **Initiation is invitation** — "I want you"
- Both spouses should initiate (Song of Solomon 7:10-12)
- Chronic refusal kills desire in the pursuing spouse
- Initiation frequency reveals who feels wanted

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand initiation as expression of desire
- Recognize the damage of chronic refusal
- Practice mutual initiation

**Assignment:**
- Couples: Track who initiates sex for 30 days
- Discuss: What pattern do we see? Who feels pursued? Who feels burdened?

**Exam:** 10 questions on initiation and pursuit

---

### **Week 9: Chapter 8 — Sexual Frequency (1 Cor 7:5 & "Defraud Ye Not")**
**Core Question:** How often should married couples have sex?

**Key Concepts:**
- 1 Cor 7:5 — "Defraud ye not" (don't deprive each other)
- Frequency is **negotiated between spouses**, not prescribed by law
- Low frequency often signals relational disconnection
- High frequency doesn't guarantee intimacy (can be duty without desire)

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Define "defrauding" in sexual context
- Understand the link between frequency and marital health
- Identify your marriage's healthy frequency rhythm

**Assignment:**
- Calculate your current frequency (monthly average)
- Reflection: Are both spouses satisfied with this rhythm? If not, what needs to change?

**Exam:** 10 questions on sexual frequency and 1 Cor 7

---

### **Week 10: Chapter 9 — Sexual Technique (Practical Instruction)**
**Core Question:** How do we grow in sexual skill and competence?

**Key Concepts:**
- Technique matters — sex is learned, not instinctive
- Communication during sex (what works, what doesn't)
- Male/female arousal differences (speed, triggers, pathways)
- **Proverbs 5:19** — "Let her breasts satisfy thee *at all times*" (repeated engagement)

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand sex as learned skill, not natural instinct
- Practice communication during sex (not just after)
- Recognize arousal differences and adapt

**Assignment:**
- Read Song of Solomon 4-7 (poetic instruction in sexual technique)
- Couples: Discuss one thing you'd like to try or improve

**Exam:** 10 questions on sexual technique and skill development

---

### **Week 11: Chapter 10 — Sexual Discipleship (Raising the Next Generation)**
**Core Question:** How do we teach our children about sex?

**Key Concepts:**
- Sexual discipleship begins at **age-appropriate levels** (not "the talk" at 13)
- Teach anatomy, design, purposes progressively
- Model healthy marriage (they learn more from watching than lectures)
- Preparing sons/daughters for covenant sex (not just "don't do it")

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Develop age-appropriate sexual education plan
- Understand what children need to know at each stage
- Prepare children for sexual literacy, not just abstinence

**Assignment:**
- Write out your sexual discipleship plan for your children (current or future)
- Reflection: What will you teach that your parents didn't?

**Exam:** 10 questions on sexual discipleship

---

### **Week 12: Chapter 11 — Conclusion & Integration (Living the Design)**
**Core Question:** How do we sustain sexual flourishing long-term?

**Key Concepts:**
- Formation over information — daily/weekly practices
- Sexual liturgies (rhythms of initiation, frequency, creativity)
- Covenant superiority over porn/counterfeits
- Accountability and ongoing learning

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Build a sustainable sexual formation plan
- Identify ongoing resources (books, courses, counseling)
- Commit to lifelong sexual discipleship

**Assignment:**
- Write your 90-day sexual formation plan
- Identify accountability partner or mentor couple
- Choose one SBD companion resource to continue learning

**Final Exam:** 20 comprehensive questions covering all 12 weeks
