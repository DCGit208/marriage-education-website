## INTRODUCTION: FROM WHO YOU ARE TO HOW YOU SEE

In Week 1, you discovered your identity: **you were created teleios** — whole, complete, fit for purpose.

In Week 2, you examined your **Four Integrated Faculties** — the powers you possess: Conative (will), Cognitive (mind), Affective (heart), and Psychomotor (embodiment).

In Week 3, you mapped the **Four Attributes of Personal Development** — the domains in which you mature: Behavioral, Temperamental, Emotional, and Mental.

Now, in **Week 4**, you learn **how to read reality accurately** — because maturity is not just about *who you are* or *what you possess*; it's about **how you discern**.

The teleios person doesn't just *feel* truth; they **apprehend** it.  
They don't just *have* values; they **order** them.  
They don't just *exist*; they **understand** existence.  
They don't just *use* words; they **honor** meaning.

This week introduces the **Four Axiomatic Dimensions of Wholeness**:

1. **Epistemology** — The discipline of knowing (*How do I know what I know?*)
2. **Axiology** — The discipline of valuing (*How do I order what matters?*)
3. **Ontology** — The discipline of being (*How do I understand reality as designed?*)
4. **Etymology** — The discipline of meaning (*How do I steward language and definition?*)

These are not abstract academic categories. They are **the lenses through which mature persons navigate covenant, culture, and civilization**.

Without these four dimensions, you can be sincere — and sincerely wrong.  
You can be passionate — and passionately destructive.  
You can be committed — and committed to confusion.

**Hebrews 5:14** declares:

> "But strong meat belongeth to them that are of full age (teleios), even those who **by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil**."

The teleios person has **trained perception**. They read reality rightly — and then live accordingly.

This week equips you to become a person who **discerns before deciding**, who **weighs before choosing**, and who **understands before acting**.

## SECTION 1: EPISTEMOLOGY — THE DISCIPLINE OF KNOWING

### 1.1 What Is Epistemology?

**Definition:**  
Epistemology is the branch of philosophy that asks: *How do we know what we know?*

It is the study of:
- **Knowledge** (what counts as true knowledge vs. opinion)
- **Belief** (how we form convictions)
- **Justification** (what grounds make a claim reliable)
- **Truth** (correspondence between claim and reality)

For the teleios person, epistemology is not optional philosophy — it is **covenant necessity**.

**Why?**  
Because **marriage demands epistemological maturity**. If you cannot discern truth from error, you cannot build trust. If you cannot distinguish between Scripture and culture, you cannot lead or follow faithfully.

### 1.2 Biblical Foundation: Sound Apprehension of Truth

**Hebrews 5:14:**  
> "By reason of use have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil."

The teleios person has **trained senses** — meaning their faculties of perception (spiritual, intellectual, moral) have been disciplined through practice.

**2 Timothy 2:15:**  
> "Study to shew thyself approved unto God, a workman that needeth not to be ashamed, rightly dividing the word of truth."

To "rightly divide" is epistemological competence: the ability to distinguish, categorize, and apply truth accurately.

**Proverbs 14:15:**  
> "The simple believeth every word: but the prudent man looketh well to his going."

The immature person accepts every claim without scrutiny. The mature person **verifies, weighs, and tests**.

### 1.3 Epistemological Immaturity: How It Shows Up

**In Relationships:**
- Believing feelings = facts ("I feel unloved, therefore I am unloved")
- Conflating perception with reality ("It seems like you don't care, so you don't care")
- Refusing correction ("That's just your interpretation")

**In Decision-Making:**
- Making choices based on trends, not truth
- Accepting pop psychology as doctrine
- Confusing sentiment with Scripture

**In Conflict:**
- "I know what you meant" (mind-reading instead of listening)
- "Everyone says..." (appeal to majority instead of principle)
- "It feels wrong, so it is wrong" (emotion as epistemology)

**Result:**  
Relationships built on assumptions collapse under reality. Covenants built on feelings fracture under pressure.

### 1.4 Epistemological Maturity: The Teleios Standard

The teleios person:

1. **Distinguishes between source and noise**  
   - Scripture > culture  
   - Principle > preference  
   - Truth > trend  

2. **Verifies before accepting**  
   - "Is this claim grounded in Scripture?"  
   - "Does this align with covenant theology?"  
   - "Is this wisdom or worldliness?"  

3. **Asks: *How do I know what I know?***  
   - "Did I learn this from the Word, or from the world?"  
   - "Am I reasoning from truth, or reacting from emotion?"  
   - "Can I defend this conviction, or am I repeating it?"  

4. **Practices interpretive humility**  
   - "I may be wrong — let me test this."  
   - "My experience is not universal law."  
   - "I will submit my judgment to Scripture."  

### 1.5 Epistemology in Marriage

**The Immature Spouse:**
- Interprets silence as rejection
- Assumes motives without asking
- Treats feelings as facts
- Resists accountability ("You don't understand me")

**The Teleios Spouse:**
- Asks before assuming
- Clarifies before accusing
- Distinguishes between impression and intent
- Submits perception to Scripture and covenant

**Example:**

| Scenario | Immature Response | Teleios Response |
|----------|-------------------|------------------|
| Spouse seems distant | "You don't love me anymore." | "I notice you've been quiet. What's on your mind?" |
| Disagreement arises | "You always do this." | "Help me understand your perspective." |
| Conviction challenged | "That's just your interpretation!" | "Let's test this against Scripture together." |

### 1.6 Daily Practice: The Epistemological Audit

Each evening, ask yourself:

1. **What did I believe today?**  
   (List 3 convictions you acted on)

2. **How do I know it's true?**  
   (Scripture? Experience? Culture? Feeling?)

3. **Did I verify before I decided?**  
   (Or did I assume, react, or repeat?)

4. **Where was my epistemology weak?**  
   (Where did I confuse feeling with fact? Opinion with truth?)

5. **What will I correct tomorrow?**  
   (One area where I will ask, verify, or test before acting)

## SECTION 2: AXIOLOGY — THE DISCIPLINE OF VALUING

### 2.1 What Is Axiology?

**Definition:**  
Axiology is the branch of philosophy that studies **value** and **value judgment** — the ordering of what matters.

It asks:
- What is good?
- What is better?
- What is best?
- How do I weigh competing goods?

For the teleios person, axiology means **rightly ordering goods** — not by trend, but by truth.

### 2.2 Biblical Foundation: The Order of Loves

**Matthew 22:37-39:**  
> "Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind. This is the first and great commandment. And the second is like unto it, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself."

There is an **order** to love:  
1. God first  
2. Neighbor second  
3. Self (rightly loved as image-bearer)

**Philippians 1:9-10:**  
> "And this I pray, that your love may abound yet more and more in knowledge and in all judgment; That ye may approve things that are excellent…"

Love is not enough. Love must be **ordered by knowledge and judgment** — meaning axiological maturity.

**Luke 14:26:**  
> "If any man come to me, and hate not his father, and mother, and wife, and children, and brethren, and sisters, yea, and his own life also, he cannot be my disciple."

This is not literal hatred — it is **axiological hierarchy**: God > all human loves. When loves compete, truth determines the order.

### 2.3 Axiological Immaturity: How It Shows Up

**In Relationships:**
- Elevating feelings above faithfulness ("I don't feel in love anymore")
- Prioritizing comfort over covenant ("This is too hard")
- Choosing preference over principle ("But I want this")

**In Decision-Making:**
- Following trends instead of truth
- Weighing opinions by popularity, not by Scripture
- Pursuing what's convenient, not what's covenantal

**In Conflict:**
- "My happiness matters most" (self as highest value)
- "If it feels wrong, it is wrong" (emotion as axiological standard)
- "Everyone else is doing it" (majority as moral measure)

**Result:**  
Relationships become transactional. Commitments become conditional. Covenants collapse when feelings shift.

### 2.4 Axiological Maturity: The Teleios Standard

The teleios person:

1. **Orders goods by truth, not by trend**  
   - Scripture > sentiment  
   - Covenant > convenience  
   - Faithfulness > feelings  

2. **Weighs competing values rightly**  
   - "Is this good, or is this best?"  
   - "Am I choosing comfort or character?"  
   - "Does this serve my covenant or my preferences?"  

3. **Resists cultural hierarchy**  
   - Culture says: Authenticity = indulgence  
   - Scripture says: Authenticity = alignment with truth  

4. **Practices sacrificial valuation**  
   - "What must I surrender for what I should steward?"  
   - "Am I willing to value what God values, even when it costs me?"  

### 2.5 Axiology in Marriage

**The Immature Spouse:**
- "I deserve to be happy" (self as ultimate value)
- "If I'm not fulfilled, I shouldn't stay" (feelings > faithfulness)
- "My needs matter more than my vows" (preference > promise)

**The Teleios Spouse:**
- "My covenant matters more than my comfort"
- "I value what God values, even when it's costly"
- "My happiness is not my highest good — holiness is"

**Example:**

| Scenario | Immature Response | Teleios Response |
|----------|-------------------|------------------|
| Marriage feels hard | "Maybe we're not meant to be." | "Covenant is costly. I will keep my vow." |
| Attraction to someone else | "I can't help how I feel." | "My feelings don't dictate my fidelity." |
| Spouse disappoints | "You're not meeting my needs." | "How can I serve you, even when I'm disappointed?" |

### 2.6 Daily Practice: The Axiological Hierarchy Chart

Each evening, ask yourself:

1. **What did I value most today?**  
   (List 3 priorities you acted on)

2. **What should I have valued most?**  
   (Scripture's order: God, covenant, others, self)

3. **Where did I invert the hierarchy?**  
   (Where did I prioritize comfort over covenant? Preference over principle?)

4. **What will I reorder tomorrow?**  
   (One area where I will align my values with truth)

## SECTION 3: ONTOLOGY — THE DISCIPLINE OF BEING

### 3.1 What Is Ontology?

**Definition:**  
Ontology is the branch of philosophy that studies **being** — the nature of existence, reality, and design.

It asks:
- What is real?
- What is the nature of things?
- How is reality structured?
- What is the design, and how do I honor it?

For the teleios person, ontology means **understanding existence and systems** — especially marriage as a **divine system**, not a human experiment.

### 3.2 Biblical Foundation: Honoring Design

**Genesis 1:27:**  
> "So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them."

Marriage is not a cultural construct. It is an **ontological reality** — a divinely designed system rooted in the nature of being itself.

**Matthew 19:4-6:**  
> "Have ye not read, that he which made them at the beginning made them male and female, And said, For this cause shall a man leave father and mother, and shall cleave to his wife: and they twain shall be one flesh? Wherefore they are no more twain, but one flesh. What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder."

Marriage is **ontological union** — not merely legal or emotional, but **being-level** fusion. It is a one-flesh reality that cannot be undone by paperwork or preference.

**Ephesians 5:31-32:**  
> "For this cause shall a man leave his father and mother, and shall be joined unto his wife, and they two shall be one flesh. This is a great mystery: but I speak concerning Christ and the church."

Marriage is **typological ontology** — it mirrors the Christ-Church union. To dishonor marriage is to distort the gospel itself.

### 3.3 Ontological Immaturity: How It Shows Up

**In Relationships:**
- Treating marriage as a contract, not a covenant
- Believing love is a feeling, not an ontological bond
- Assuming "We can redefine marriage" (rebellion against design)

**In Decision-Making:**
- "If I don't feel married, I'm not married" (emotion over ontology)
- "Marriage is whatever we make it" (subjectivism over structure)
- "We can end this if it doesn't work" (disposability over permanence)

**In Conflict:**
- "This isn't working" (functionality over fidelity)
- "I need to find myself first" (individualism over union)
- "We're just incompatible" (preference over design)

**Result:**  
Covenants are treated as contracts. Ontological realities are reduced to emotional preferences. Marriage becomes an experiment, not a sanctuary.

### 3.4 Ontological Maturity: The Teleios Standard

The teleios person:

1. **Honors design over desire**  
   - Marriage is what God made it, not what I feel it should be  
   - One-flesh union is ontological, not optional  

2. **Recognizes covenant as being-level reality**  
   - "We are one flesh" (not "we feel connected")  
   - "This is permanent" (not "this is pleasant")  

3. **Resists cultural redefinition**  
   - Culture says: Marriage = personal fulfillment  
   - Scripture says: Marriage = covenant ontology  

4. **Practices ontological alignment**  
   - "Am I living according to what marriage IS, or what I want it to be?"  
   - "Do I honor the structure, or do I resent it?"  

### 3.5 Ontology in Marriage

**The Immature Spouse:**
- "If I don't feel married, I'm not married"
- "We can redefine this relationship"
- "Marriage is what we make it"

**The Teleios Spouse:**
- "Marriage is what God made it — I submit to the design"
- "We are one flesh, regardless of how I feel"
- "This covenant is ontological, not optional"

**Example:**

| Scenario | Immature Response | Teleios Response |
|----------|-------------------|------------------|
| Marriage feels empty | "Maybe this wasn't meant to be." | "Covenant is not based on feeling. We are one flesh." |
| Cultural pressure | "Maybe marriage should evolve." | "Marriage is designed by God. I honor the structure." |
| Desire to redefine | "We'll do marriage our way." | "We submit to God's design, not our preferences." |

### 3.6 Daily Practice: The Ontological Alignment Audit

Each evening, ask yourself:

1. **Did I honor design today?**  
   (Did I treat marriage as covenant, or as convenience?)

2. **Did I resist redefinition?**  
   (Did I align with Scripture, or with culture?)

3. **Did I live from being, or from feeling?**  
   (Did I act according to what marriage IS, or what I feel?)

4. **What will I align tomorrow?**  
   (One area where I will honor ontology over emotion)

## SECTION 4: ETYMOLOGY — THE DISCIPLINE OF MEANING

### 4.1 What Is Etymology?

**Definition:**  
Etymology is the study of the **origin and history of words** — the discipline of meaning, semantics, and definition.

It asks:
- What does this word actually mean?
- Where did this definition come from?
- How has meaning shifted over time?
- Am I using language rightly, or carelessly?

For the teleios person, etymology is **the stewardship of language** — because **words name realities**. When meaning is lost, definition disintegrates — and society soon follows.

### 4.2 Biblical Foundation: Words as Realities

**John 1:1:**  
> "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God."

Language is not arbitrary. The **Word** (Logos) is foundational to reality itself.

**Proverbs 18:21:**  
> "Death and life are in the power of the tongue: and they that love it shall eat the fruit thereof."

Words are not neutral. They **create or destroy**, build or break, clarify or confuse.

**Matthew 5:37:**  
> "But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay: for whatsoever is more than these cometh of evil."

Teleios communication is **precise**. Ambiguity is the language of deception.

**James 3:2:**  
> "If any man offend not in word, the same is a perfect (teleios) man, and able also to bridle the whole body."

The teleios person masters language — not to manipulate, but to **honor meaning**.

### 4.3 Etymological Immaturity: How It Shows Up

**In Relationships:**
- Redefining words to escape accountability ("That's not what I meant")
- Using vague language to avoid commitment ("We'll see")
- Shifting definitions mid-conflict ("When I said 'always,' I didn't mean literally")

**In Decision-Making:**
- Accepting culture's redefinitions without scrutiny (e.g., "love," "marriage," "truth")
- Using words emotionally, not definitionally ("I feel like 'always' is too strong")
- Refusing precision ("Why does it matter what the word means?")

**In Conflict:**
- "You're being too literal" (rejecting precision)
- "Words don't matter that much" (devaluing meaning)
- "That's just semantics" (dismissing etymology)

**Result:**  
Covenants collapse because words lose weight. Vows become suggestions. Promises become preferences.

### 4.4 Etymological Maturity: The Teleios Standard

The teleios person:

1. **Honors the weight of words**  
   - "I said it, therefore I will do it"  
   - "My words mean what they mean"  

2. **Resists cultural redefinition**  
   - Culture says: "Love = feeling"  
   - Scripture says: "Love = covenant action"  

3. **Practices etymological precision**  
   - "What does this word actually mean?"  
   - "Am I using language rightly, or lazily?"  

4. **Stewards meaning as sacred**  
   - "Words name realities. I will not distort them."  

### 4.5 Etymology in Marriage

**The Immature Spouse:**
- "I said 'forever,' but I didn't mean literally"
- "Words don't matter as much as feelings"
- "You're being too literal"

**The Teleios Spouse:**
- "I said 'forever' — and I meant it"
- "My words carry weight. I will honor them."
- "Precision matters. Ambiguity breeds confusion."

**Example:**

| Scenario | Immature Response | Teleios Response |
|----------|-------------------|------------------|
| Vow feels hard | "When I said 'forever,' I meant 'as long as I feel it.'" | "I said 'forever.' I will keep my word." |
| Definition challenged | "That's just semantics!" | "Words mean what they mean. Let's clarify." |
| Cultural pressure | "Everyone redefines love now." | "I define love by Scripture, not by culture." |

### 4.6 Daily Practice: The Etymological Stewardship Audit

Each evening, ask yourself:

1. **What did I promise today?**  
   (List 3 commitments you made)

2. **Did I honor the weight of my words?**  
   (Did I keep my word, or did I redefine it?)

3. **Did I resist cultural redefinition?**  
   (Did I accept culture's language, or Scripture's?)

4. **What will I clarify tomorrow?**  
   (One area where I will honor meaning over ambiguity)

## SECTION 5: INTEGRATION — THE FOUR DIMENSIONS WORKING TOGETHER

The Four Axiomatic Dimensions are not isolated categories. They are **integrated lenses** through which the teleios person reads reality.

### 5.1 How the Four Dimensions Interact

| Dimension | Question | Covenant Application |
|-----------|----------|----------------------|
| **Epistemology** | *How do I know?* | "Is my conviction grounded in Scripture or sentiment?" |
| **Axiology** | *What do I value?* | "Am I ordering goods by truth or by trend?" |
| **Ontology** | *What is real?* | "Am I honoring marriage as designed, or as desired?" |
| **Etymology** | *What do words mean?* | "Am I stewarding language rightly, or carelessly?" |

### 5.2 Case Study: The Four Dimensions in Action

**Scenario:** You feel disconnected from your spouse. You're considering whether to "take a break" to "find yourself."

**Epistemological Question:**  
*How do I know this disconnection is a sign to leave?*  
- Immature: "I feel it, so it's true."  
- Teleios: "Is this conviction grounded in Scripture? Or am I reacting emotionally?"

**Axiological Question:**  
*What do I value most — my comfort or my covenant?*  
- Immature: "My happiness matters most."  
- Teleios: "My covenant matters more than my comfort."

**Ontological Question:**  
*What is the reality of my marriage?*  
- Immature: "If I don't feel married, I'm not married."  
- Teleios: "We are one flesh. This is ontological, not emotional."

**Etymological Question:**  
*What does 'taking a break' actually mean?*  
- Immature: "It's just semantics."  
- Teleios: "'Taking a break' = retreat from covenant. Words matter."

**Result:**  
The teleios person **discerns rightly** — and chooses covenant over convenience.

### 5.3 Integration Practice: The Four-Lens Audit

Before any major decision, ask:

1. **Epistemology:** How do I know this is true?
2. **Axiology:** What am I valuing most?
3. **Ontology:** What is the reality of this situation?
4. **Etymology:** What do my words actually mean?

## SECTION 6: ERRORS TO REJECT

### 6.1 Error #1: "Thinking Doesn't Matter — Feelings Do"

**Culture says:** "Follow your heart. Trust your gut."  
**Scripture says:** "The heart is deceitful above all things" (Jeremiah 17:9).

**Teleios response:** Feelings are real, but they are not always true. The teleios person **tests feelings against truth**.

### 6.2 Error #2: "Words Don't Matter — Intent Does"

**Culture says:** "It's not what you said, it's what you meant."  
**Scripture says:** "Let your yea be yea, and your nay be nay" (James 5:12).

**Teleios response:** Words carry weight. Ambiguity is the language of deception.

### 6.3 Error #3: "Marriage Is What We Make It"

**Culture says:** "Define your own relationship."  
**Scripture says:** "What God hath joined together, let not man put asunder" (Matthew 19:6).

**Teleios response:** Marriage is designed by God. We submit to the structure; we don't redefine it.

### 6.4 Error #4: "Values Are Subjective"

**Culture says:** "Your truth is your truth; my truth is my truth."  
**Scripture says:** "Thy word is truth" (John 17:17).

**Teleios response:** Truth is objective. Values are ordered by Scripture, not by sentiment.
