## The Central Truth

The **conative faculty** is the capacity of commitment and striving action. It concerns directed volition: not merely what you know or feel, but the capacity through which intention is committed toward action.

You do not acquire conation and thereby become a person. You are **teleios** by design; the conative faculty belongs to your designed human architecture. Education makes you mindful of it. Responsibility requires you to espouse it and live accordingly. The car drives because it is a car. Committed action expresses a capacity already proper to the person; action does not manufacture the person or teleios maturity. A spouse is better whole — not borrowed will.

## Core Teaching

### 1) Faculty, not attribute

| Conative capacity | Behavioral attribute |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| Power/capacity of commitment and striving action | Personal-development attribute of self-control through action and reaction |
| Answers: *What power directs willing toward action?* | Answers: *How is self-possession expressed in conduct?* |
| Course 04 | Course 02 Week 3 |

They interact, but they are not interchangeable. Conation is not simply “good behavior.” A person may behave politely while refusing meaningful commitment; another may possess strong striving action but direct it irresponsibly. Attributes ≠ Faculties — keep the map clean.

### 2) Teleios grammar (non-negotiable)

| Manufacture / chase grammar | Practice-from-teleios grammar |
|-----------------------------|-------------------------------|
| “Willpower will make me a real adult” | “I am teleios; I steward conation” |
| Streak as ontology meter | Rails as stewardship meter |
| Partner as outsourced will | Better whole — I remain first captain of commitment |
| Practice invents capacity | Practice reveals responsible operation |

### 3) The conative sequence

1. **Apprehension** — I recognize an object, duty, or course.  
2. **Valuation** — I judge it worthy of commitment.  
3. **Intention** — I direct myself toward it.  
4. **Commitment** — I bind decision to responsibility.  
5. **Striving action** — I move and persist in accord with commitment.  

This sequence does not create conative capacity. It makes its operation visible.

### 4) Conative refusal

Conative refusal commonly appears as: endless intention without commitment; commitment announced but repeatedly outsourced; enthusiasm dependent on applause; surrender of agency to mood, fear, or another person; “better half” expectations — *someone else will provide the will I refuse to own*.

The correction is not “become a new human through willpower.” The correction is: **be mindful of conative design, accept accountability, and live accordingly**.

### 5) Commitment is not stubbornness

Striving action must remain governed by design, reason, and value. Stubbornness can persist in error. Conative responsibility includes revising a course when cognitive evidence or moral order shows it wrong.

Therefore: commitment without cognition can become zeal without knowledge; without affective care, domination; without psychomotor readiness, fantasy; without Behavioral self-possession, inconsistency. Integration begins now; each capacity receives its own week.

### 6) Covenant relevance

Covenant is not sustained by feeling alone. It requires the person to mean, choose, and stand within commitment. Yet commitment does not make a half-person whole. Marriage unites two whole persons. Your spouse is better whole, not borrowed conation. “You make me commit” is as anthropologically disordered as “you complete me.”

### 7) Ratiocination

- Premise 1: What a person *is* precedes what a person *practices*.  
- Premise 2: Conative drills that assume unfinished ontology become self-creation religion.  
- Premise 3: Outsourcing will to a partner denies teleios and better whole.  
- Conclusion: Steward commitment from design; do not manufacture personhood or borrow a half’s will.

### 8) Four-column costs of conative refusal

When intention never binds, inventory costs: Body · Household · Work/calling · Inner life. If you cannot name costs, you are still romanticizing drift as “waiting on God” or “waiting for motivation.”

### 9) Language discipline

- “I’m waiting to feel ready.” → “I am teleios; I will commit to ______ by ______.”  
- “You keep me accountable forever.” → “Witnesses help; I remain captain of my will.”  
- “This drill will make me disciplined enough to be whole.” → “I am already teleios; this drill aligns expression.”

### 10) Common errors this week

1. Calling the conative faculty the Behavioral attribute  
2. Treating willpower as the source of personhood  
3. Confusing stubbornness with commitment  
4. Using “I am waiting for motivation” to outsource responsibility  
5. Recruiting a spouse as will, regulator, or missing half  
6. Believing a seven-day practice manufactures maturity  
7. Softening better-whole speech while outsourcing agency

### 11) Continuity into Week 2

Commitment without learning/reasoning becomes zeal without knowledge. Week 2 installs Cognitive stewardship so premises govern striving.

### 12) Formation path reminder

Mindful → educated → espouse design → live accordingly. Training in the course title means education and responsible stewardship — not creation of the faculty.

### 13) What this week is not

Not hustle religion. Not permission for coercive stubbornness. Not anti-feeling. Not “manifest willpower.” It is anti-**wrong-rooted** striving and anti-will outsourcing.

## Case Contrasts

### Case A — Intention without conation expressed
Daniel speaks passionately about marriage, enterprise, faith, and service. Every plan remains “soon.” He calls this waiting for the right feeling. The issue is not missing ontology; it is refusal to bind intention to accountable action. Fruit: household distrust; romantic mythology.

### Case B — Commitment without integration / fusion
Mara commits quickly and persists fiercely—even after evidence shows harm — or outsources agency: “You make me follow through.” Persistence or fusion alone is not responsible stewardship. Fruit: harm or dependency baptized as strength.

### Case C — Conative responsibility from teleios
Joel states one commitment, gives it a time and action, invites appropriate accountability, revises when truth requires, and refuses better-half will. The sequence does not create his capacity; it reveals responsible operation. Fruit: calm reliability.

## Seven-Day Drill — Conative Responsibility Practice

| Day | Commitment named | Visible action + time | Kept / revised / refused | Outsourced agency? | Repair? |
|-----|------------------|----------------------|--------------------------|--------------------|---------|

**Purpose:** education and accountable living — not manufacturing conation or maturity.

**Morning (10 min):** (1) State: “I am teleios; conative capacity belongs to my design: commitment and striving action.” (2) Name one commitment already proper to today. (3) Specify one visible action, time, and completion condition.  
**Midday (3 min):** Still committed or merely interested? New truth requiring revision? Asking mood or another person to supply agency?  
**Evening (12 min):** Log commitment · action · deviation reason · accountability/repair.  
**Day 7:** Write one rule: *Because I am teleios and possess conative capacity by design, I am responsible to ______ when ______. I will not recruit a better half to supply my will.*
