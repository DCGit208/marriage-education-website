## The Central Truth

You are not four fragments assembled by training. You are **one teleios person** whose human architecture includes Conative, Cognitive, Psychomotor, and Affective capacities.

Integration does not create wholeness. It is the responsible, coordinated life of the whole person:

- Conative commits and strives
- Cognitive learns and reasons
- Psychomotor coordinates physical and mental action
- Affective exercises care, forethought, and empathy

The car does not become a car by driving. The teleios person does not become human by coordinating faculties. Coordination is life according to design.

## Week Overview

### Core Question
How do all four capacities operate together in ordinary and covenant life?

### Key Insight
No faculty should impersonate the whole person. Strong commitment without reasoning can become reckless; reasoning without action becomes sterile; action without care becomes mechanical; care without commitment becomes sentiment. Responsible living coordinates all four.

### Learning Outcomes
By the end of this week you will:

- Recite and define the Four Faculties accurately
- Distinguish capacities from attributes and skills
- Map one real decision through all four capacities
- Write a Four Faculties Stewardship Charter

## Core Teaching

### 1) The authoritative distinction

| Category | Canon list | Function |
|----------|------------|----------|
| Skills | Critical Thinking, Expression, Writing, Hearing, Reading | Learned competencies |
| Attributes | Behavioral, Temperamental, Emotional, Mental | Attributes of personal development/personality |
| Capacities / Faculties | Conative, Cognitive, Psychomotor, Affective | Powers/capacities of the person |
| Maturation pillars | Etymology, Axiology, Epistemology, Ontology | Axiomatic dimensions/lenses |

If you cannot preserve these categories, return to the Personal Development Maturation Map before advancing.

### 2) The integration sequence

For a real duty:

1. **Cognitive:** What is true? What must I learn or reason?
2. **Affective:** Who is affected? What care, forethought, and empathy are required?
3. **Conative:** What commitment must I make?
4. **Psychomotor:** What physical/mental coordination will execute it?
5. **Review:** Does the action agree with design and covenant responsibility?

This is not always chronological. The capacities interact dynamically. The sequence is an educational tool.

### 3) Faculty dominance and faculty neglect

| Pattern | Likely fruit |
|---------|--------------|
| Conative dominates | force, stubbornness, action without listening |
| Cognitive dominates | analysis without commitment or embodiment |
| Psychomotor dominates | performance/output without reason or care |
| Affective dominates | concern without decision, boundaries, or truth |

The goal is not equal minutes for each faculty. The goal is appropriate integration for the duty.

### 4) Integration and the Four Attributes

Faculties and attributes interact:

- Conative capacity may be expressed through Behavioral self-command.
- Cognitive capacity interacts with the Mental attribute.
- Affective capacity must not be collapsed into the Emotional attribute.
- Psychomotor action may be visible behavior without becoming the Behavioral attribute.

There is no simple one-to-one replacement chart. Keep ontology disciplined.

### 5) Integration and covenant

Consider a difficult marital conversation:

- **Cognitive:** apprehend facts; distinguish interpretation
- **Affective:** consider impact; empathize without surrendering truth
- **Conative:** commit to the conversation and repair
- **Psychomotor:** remain present, regulate pace, speak, listen, act

None of this means the conversation manufactures maturity. It is the responsibility of mature persons living their capacities.

The spouse is not your missing faculty. “You think for me,” “you motivate me,” “you make me act,” or “you make me care” are variations of better-half anthropology. Better whole means each person owns their design while joining in one-flesh covenant.

### 6) Hebrews 5:14 correctly read

Those “of full age” have senses exercised in the course of use. The verse identifies the persons as being of full age; it does not say activity manufactures their ontology.

Therefore:

- maturity = state of being mature / full development
- use = life, responsibility, accountability
- education = becoming mindful of design and able to live accordingly
- practice = expression, not creation, of personhood

## Four Faculties Stewardship Charter

Write one rule per faculty and three integration rules:

1. **Conative:** Because I possess commitment/striving capacity, I will…
2. **Cognitive:** Because I possess learning/reasoning capacity, I will…
3. **Psychomotor:** Because I possess physical/mental agility, I will…
4. **Affective:** Because I possess care/forethought/empathy, I will…
5. **Category rule:** I will not confuse faculties with…
6. **Ontology rule:** Practice does not…
7. **Covenant rule:** Better whole means…

## Common Errors

1. Returning to the incorrect attribute-as-faculty list
2. Treating integration as equal performance rather than appropriate coordination
3. Using drills to claim superiority or manufacture maturity
4. Ignoring accessibility and contextual expression
5. Asking a partner to supply a missing faculty
6. Calling strong output “wholeness”
