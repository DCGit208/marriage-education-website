## The Central Truth

The **cognitive faculty** is the capacity for learning and reasoning. It is not merely the storage of information, academic credentials, or quick intelligence. It is the human power through which knowledge is apprehended, premises are examined, distinctions are made, and conclusions are reasoned.

You do not study and thereby become cognitive. You learn because cognitive capacity belongs to human design. Education brings that capacity into conscious and responsible operation.

## Week Overview

### Core Question
How do learning and reasoning serve design rather than preference?

### Key Insight
Cognition is not permission to invent reality. Its responsibility is to apprehend and reason from what is: revelation, creation design, and fruit/history. Isaiah 1:18—“Come now, and let us reason together”—calls the person into responsible thought.

## Core Teaching

### 1) Cognitive capacity is not the Mental attribute

| Cognitive capacity | Mental attribute |
|--------------------|------------------|
| Learning and reasoning | Development of the mind/faculty of reason and intellectual processes |
| A capacity/power of the person | An attribute of personal development |
| Course 04 | Course 02 Week 3 |

The concepts relate, but category discipline matters. Cognitive names a faculty. Mental names an attribute of the human personality. Calling every thought “cognitive development” erases the map.

### 2) The cognitive sequence

1. **Attend** — give the subject honest attention.
2. **Apprehend** — understand what is being claimed.
3. **Identify premises** — what must be true for the claim to stand?
4. **Test witnesses** — revelation, creation design, fruit/history.
5. **Reason** — determine whether the conclusion follows.
6. **Judge responsibly** — accept, reject, revise, or suspend.

This sequence does not manufacture cognition. It disciplines responsible use.

### 3) Design precedes preference

When culture says, “follow your truth,” Cognitive responsibility asks:

- What is the ontology?
- What does creation disclose?
- What fruit follows?
- Is this truth, interpretation, preference, or trend?

Preference is allowed to be preference. It is not allowed to masquerade as anthropology.

### 4) Cognitive distortions

Common refusals include:

- **confirmation** — only hearing what protects the preferred conclusion
- **rationalization** — constructing reasons after desire has already ruled
- **hearsay** — repeating claims never examined
- **category collapse** — calling attributes faculties, mood identity, or practice ontology
- **false equation** — assuming “I feel incomplete” proves “I am incomplete”

The “better half” slogan is a cognitive error before it becomes a relationship burden. It starts with a false premise: *I am ontologically half*. Correct premise: humanity is created teleios. Correct conclusion: marriage unites two wholes in one flesh.

### 5) Cognition and covenant

Covenant requires more than clever argument. Cognitive responsibility serves truth, not victory. In conflict, cognition should help distinguish:

- event from interpretation
- intent from impact
- evidence from suspicion
- design from preference
- covenant fact from romantic mythology

A spouse is not an opponent to outreason. A better whole brings another whole perspective into responsible dialogue.

## Case Contrast

### Information without reasoning
Amara has read many books and can quote specialists. She cannot explain the premises beneath her conclusions. Information has become decoration.

### Reasoning captured by desire
Peter wants a relationship to work, so every warning is reclassified as “fear.” Cognition is serving preference rather than truth.

### Cognitive responsibility
Lina states the claim, tests premises, listens accurately, and changes her conclusion when evidence requires. Revision is not weakness; it is accountability.

## Seven-Day Responsibility Practice

### Morning (10 minutes)

1. State: “Cognitive capacity belongs to my design: learning and reasoning.”
2. Select one claim you expect to encounter today.
3. Write its likely premise.

### Midday (5 minutes)

Ask:

- What do I know?
- What am I interpreting?
- What do I merely prefer?
- Which witness have I ignored?

### Evening (15 minutes)

Record:

- one thing learned
- one premise examined
- one conclusion revised or confirmed
- one responsibility created by the truth

### Day 7

Write your Cognitive Stewardship Rule:

> Because I possess Cognitive capacity by design, I will reason from ______ rather than ______.

## Common Errors

1. Calling Cognitive capacity the Mental attribute
2. Treating information accumulation as wisdom
3. Using intelligence to dominate rather than apprehend truth
4. Treating preference as ontology
5. Believing study manufactures personhood or maturity
6. Reasoning from “better half” feelings rather than teleios design
