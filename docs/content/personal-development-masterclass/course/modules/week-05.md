# Week 5: Temperament Training
## Stewarding Default Drift Without Excusing It

> **Week 5 of 12** | 8–10 hours | Attribute Stewardship  
> **Sources:** Course 02 Week 3 (Temperamental attribute) · Course 05 maturation laws · Proverbs 25:28 · Galatians 5:22–23 · Isaiah 1:18

## The Central Truth

Temperament is **not destiny**. Temperament is **default drift** in the Temperamental attribute — how energy, pace, intensity, and relational style tend to move unless governed.

You do not become teleios by changing your temperament brand. You are teleios by design. You are responsible to steward drift so likeness appears in ordinary life: predictable virtue, not personality as excuse.

---

## Week Overview

### Core Question
How is temperament stewarded, not excused?

### Key Insight
Culture sells temperament as identity (“I’m just an introvert / fiery / chill — deal with it”). Canon treats temperament as a **personal-development attribute** to be educated and governed — like Mental and Emotional — not as ontology and not as a license to harm household order.

### Learning Outcomes
By the end of this week you will:

- Define the Temperamental attribute without confusing it with identity or with Behavioral self-control
- Name your default drift with evidence (not with pop labels alone)
- Install one counter-habit that protects covenant fruit
- Complete a Temperament Stewardship Audit
- Write one Temperament Covenant Rule and a 5-day partner check-in log

## Core Teaching

### 1) Attribute, not brand, not ontology

| | Temperamental attribute | What it is not |
|--|-------------------------|----------------|
| Layer | Personal development / personality expression | Image of God / teleios being |
| Question | How do pace, intensity, withdrawal, and engagement tend to run? | Who am I? |
| Failure | Excuse, contempt for opposite styles, unpredictability baptized as “personality” | “I need a new temperament to be whole” |

You may describe patterns (quiet under load, fast under conflict, warm in public / cold at home). You may not say “this pattern *is* me” as if design ends at preference.

Ratiocination:

- Premise 1: What is given as image cannot be reduced to a temperament type.
- Premise 2: What repeatedly damages trust is not “authenticity”; it is ungoverned drift.
- Conclusion: Temperament training is stewardship of expression, not reinvention of being.

### 2) Default drift vs Behavioral attribute

Keep Course 02’s map clean:

- **Temperamental** — characteristic tendency of energy and style
- **Behavioral** — self-control through action and reaction
- **Emotional** — how feeling surges and is governed (Week 4)
- **Mental** — how thought and premises run (Week 3)

A person can be temperamentally intense and Behaviorally governed — or temperamentally mild and Behaviorally chaotic. Mildness is not maturity. Intensity is not immaturity. **Governance** is the issue.

### 3) The excuse liturgy (and its replacement)

Excuse liturgy:

- “That’s just how I am.”
- “God made me this way, so don’t require change.”
- “If you loved me, you’d adapt to my drift.”

Replacement liturgy:

- “This is my default under pressure.”
- “Design requires fruit, not only familiarity.”
- “I will install a counter-habit so household trust is protected.”

“God made me” is true of image. It is false as a veto against likeness. Holiness remains state; likeness remains formed alignment.

### 4) Counter-habit logic

A counter-habit is not a new personality. It is a **pre-committed opposite move** at the point of drift.

Examples:

| Default drift | Covenant cost | Counter-habit |
|---------------|---------------|---------------|
| Withdraw when conflict starts | Repair delayed; spouse carries silence | Stay 10 minutes; name one true sentence |
| Escalate volume/speed | Fear replaces truth | Lower pace; one question before reply |
| Over-accommodate to keep peace | Truth unpaid | State one boundary same day |
| Perform charm in public, dump contempt at home | Split witness | Same courtesy rule indoors |

The car still drives because it is a car. The counter-habit steers; it does not manufacture the engine.

### 5) Predictable virtue

Households do not need you to be “easy.” They need you to be **predictable under design**:

- same person under fatigue as under applause
- same truthfulness when bored as when inspired
- same repair speed when you caused harm

Temperament training aims at predictability of virtue, not the erasure of style.

### 6) Analogy: weather vane vs keel

Temperament without governance is a **weather vane** — it points wherever pressure blows.  
Stewarded temperament keeps a **keel** — style may lean, but covenant direction holds.

You do not blame the wind for a ship without a keel.

### 7) Better whole applied to temperament

Better-half error here:

- “My spouse balances my temperament, so I don’t have to change.”
- “Opposites complete each other” as ontology (½ + ½).

Complementary styles can serve a household. They do not complete a person. Each person owns drift. Fusion is not formation.

### 8) Epistemic test for temperament claims

1. **Design coherence** — Does this claim reduce image to a type?
2. **Moral coherence** — Does it excuse harm?
3. **Relational fruit** — Does trust rise when I live this claim?
4. **Civilizational fruit** — Would households stabilize if everyone used “that’s just me” as law?

If a claim fails, it is fashion with a personality quiz — not anthropology.

### 9) Load test for temperament

Course 05: maturity claims must survive threshold pressure.

Temperament excuses usually appear under fatigue, delay, or disagreement. Therefore:

- Practice the counter-habit on a hard day, not only a calm day.
- Ask your partner: “When I’m tired, what drift do you actually meet?”
- Score predictability separately for low-load vs high-load days.

If virtue only appears when rested, it is mood, not stewarded temperament.

### 10) Integration with Weeks 3–4

Temperament drift often *recruits* Mental premises and Emotional surges:

- Drift: withdraw → Premise: “Conflict means I’m unsafe as a person” → Surge: shame → Action: silence for days.

Interrupt the chain at the earliest owned point. Temperament training without Weeks 3–4 becomes behavior tips with no epistemology.

## Case Contrasts

### Case A — Temperament as identity
Derek calls himself “a blunt truth-teller.” He is often cruel. He treats correction as an attack on authenticity. Drift has become a brand.

### Case B — Temperament as shame
Lila hates her quietness and tries to become loud to be “leadership material.” She confuses style with calling. She needs stewardship of quiet strength, not a personality transplant.

### Case C — Stewarded drift
Marcus names: “Under conflict I withdraw.” Counter-habit: stay seated, one clarifying question, repair same day. Style remains quieter than his wife’s; trust rises because drift is governed.

## Seven-Day Drill — Temperament Check-In

**Partner required** (spouse, friend, or accountability witness). Five check-ins minimum (Days 1–5); Days 6–7 synthesize.

### Daily (Days 1–5)

1. Name today’s dominant drift (one sentence).
2. Did the counter-habit fire? (Y/N + evidence)
3. Partner scores predictability 1–5 for the day.
4. One repair if drift harmed.

### Day 6 — Pattern map
List top three triggers that summon your worst drift.

### Day 7 — Temperament Covenant Rule

> When ______ happens, my default is ______. I will instead ______. If I fail, I will repair by ______ within ______.
