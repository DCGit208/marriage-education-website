## The Central Truth

Identity is **given**. Calling is **stewardship of what is given**.  
Neither is manufactured by branding, trauma narrative, or vocational theater.

The catalogue stub said identity is discovered, not invented, and calling is received, not manufactured. Keep that. Tighten it: discovery is education about design already true; reception is responsibility, not a mystical waiting room.

## Week Overview

### Core Question
How does given identity become covenant calling?

### Key Insight
People reverse the order. They hunt a calling to feel like someone, then call that hunt “identity work.” Canon order is: **image given → likeness formed → calling as deployment**. Marriage, work, and ministry receive a whole person; they do not assemble one.

### Learning Outcomes
By the end of this week you will:

- State identity in design language (image, holiness as state, teleios)
- Distinguish identity, likeness, role, and calling
- Name one false identity still running choices
- Write a calling sentence that does not outsource being
- Complete an Identity–Calling Alignment Audit

## Core Teaching

### 1) Identity is not a costume you find in the market

Course 03 already drilled this. The MasterClass applies it under load.

**Identity** answers: *What am I?*  
**Calling** answers: *What am I responsible to do with what I am?*

If calling is used to answer the first question, you will change names every time the job, the relationship, or the platform changes.

| Term | Canon meaning | Common counterfeit |
|------|----------------|-------------------|
| Image | Given — you bear God’s design | Selfie-ontology / brand |
| Holiness | State — *be* holy | Moral athletics to become holy |
| Likeness | Formed alignment of practice to design | Performance as identity |
| Role | Temporary office (spouse, parent, worker) | Role as the self |
| Calling | Stewardship trajectory of design in time | Destiny myth / career glamour |

### 2) “Discovered” without the invention trap

Discovery language is legitimate only if what is discovered **pre-exists** the search.

You discover a coastline. You do not discover a coastline by drawing one on the ocean.

So:

- You may discover *how* your design is to be stewarded in this season.
- You may not discover a new species of self.

Ratiocination:

- Premise 1: What is invented can be reinvented at will.
- Premise 2: Covenant requires a stable who, not an endlessly reinvented who.
- Conclusion: Identity that is invented cannot bear covenant weight.

### 3) Calling is not the missing half of identity

The better-half error appears here as:

- “When I find my calling, I will know who I am.”
- “My spouse is my calling.”
- “My children are my identity.”
- “My ministry is my being.”

A spouse may be part of how calling is lived. A spouse is not your ontology.  
Children are fruit and charge, not the source of personhood.  
Work is offering, not essence.

**Better whole:** calling is what a whole person *does*. It is not the glue that holds a half-person together.

### 4) The calling sentence — how to write one without lying

A true calling sentence has four parts:

1. **Who** — design identity (not job title)
2. **Among whom** — actual people, not abstractions only
3. **What stewardship** — visible responsibility
4. **By what constraint** — design limits (truth, fidelity, sexed nature, non-harm as covenant order)

Example (structure, not a script you must copy):

> I am a teleios man/woman by design, responsible to serve [household/community] through [truthful work and ordered care], under the constraints of fidelity, and without recruiting others to supply my will.

If your sentence cannot survive losing the platform, it was a brand, not a calling.

### 5) Faculties and attributes in calling

Calling uses the whole PD map. It does not collapse into one faculty.

- **Conative** — you commit; calling is not endless “sensing”
- **Cognitive** — you reason; calling is not anti-intellectual fog
- **Psychomotor** — you show up in body; calling is not disembodied vibe
- **Affective** — you care with forethought; calling is not cold output

Attributes (Behavioral, Temperamental, Emotional, Mental) color *how* calling is expressed. They do not rewrite *who* you are.

### 6) Analogy: instrument, not identity swap

A violin does not become a violin by being played in Carnegie Hall.  
It is a violin in the case. Playing is stewardship of what it is.

Calling is the music. Identity is the instrument.  
Do not confuse repertoire with wood.

### 7) Holiness, righteousness, and calling without moonshot theology

“Be holy, for I am holy” (Lev 11:44; 1 Pet 1:16) is state language — the copula *be*, not “become holy by career success.”  
Calling does not upgrade holiness. Holiness is who you are by design and reconciliation; calling is how that design is deployed.

Likewise, righteousness as *dikaiosynē* — “state of him who is as he ought to be” (cf. 2 Cor 5:21 lexicon) — is not a job title.  
If your calling sentence requires you to *become* acceptable before you can serve, you have smuggled religionism back into ontology. God reconciled us to design in Christ (2 Cor 5:17–18). Calling flows from that fact; it does not manufacture it.

### 8) Ratiocination: season vs instability

Claim: “I change cities and titles because God is leading me into new seasons.”

- Hidden premise: frequent relocation of role is evidence of obedience.
- Counter-evidence: likeness requires time under ordered conditions (Course 05, Law of Time); fruit needs duration.
- Design test: seasons may reassign *stewardship*; they do not rewrite *who*.
- Replacement: “I will finish likeness in the present assignment before I romanticize the next.”

### 9) False calling patterns

1. **Calling as rescue fantasy** — the next role will finally make me feel real.
2. **Calling as spiritualized avoidance** — “I’m waiting on God” while conation refuses a clear next act.
3. **Calling as comparison** — your assignment must look like someone else’s platform.
4. **Calling as household abandonment** — “ministry” that regularly violates household trust.
5. **Calling as better-half fusion** — “my spouse is my calling” as ontology, not as shared stewardship.

## Case Contrasts

### Case A — Calling as identity hunt
Chris changes cities, churches, and titles every 18 months, calling it “obedience to a new season.” The pattern is not seasons. It is refusal of likeness: unwillingness to be the same person long enough for fruit to appear.

### Case B — Role-as-self
Priya is a devoted mother who cannot complete a sentence about herself that is not about the children. When they sleep, she feels unreal. Motherhood is holy stewardship. It is not the image of God. The gap is identity collapsed into role.

### Case C — Aligned calling
Noah states identity from design, names one false identity (“I am only as good as useful”), writes a calling sentence tied to household and truthful work, and accepts a 90-day constraint: no new branding, only likeness in the current assignment.

## Seven-Day Drill — Identity then Calling

### Day 1 — Identity paragraph
200 words. No job titles. No marital status as the first sentence.

### Day 2 — False identity
Name it. Give evidence (three repeated choices). Write a replacement vow from design.

### Day 3 — Role inventory
List roles. Mark which ones you have allowed to impersonate identity.

### Day 4 — Calling sentence draft
Use the four-part structure. Read it aloud to one witness.

### Day 5 — Constraint test
Remove money and applause from the sentence. What remains?

### Day 6 — Faculty check
Where is calling stalled: conation, cognition, body, or care? One restoring action.

### Day 7 — Alignment rule
> Because identity is given, I will not use ______ as a name for myself. Because calling is stewardship, I will ______ this week.

### Platform-loss stress test

Write your calling sentence. Then rewrite it as if:

- the title is gone,
- the income is halved,
- the audience is zero,
- the marital status is unchanged as ontology (married or single does not rewrite image).

If nothing durable remains, you wrote a brand brief, not a calling.
