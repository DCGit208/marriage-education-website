## The Central Truth

Forgiveness is a **structural act**, not a feeling.  
Bridge repair restores load-bearing capacity. Feelings may lag. Mastery refuses both: cheap “I forgive you” fog and permanent prosecution as identity.

---

## Week Overview

### Core Question
How do repair and forgiveness work as structure?

### Key Insight
A repair backlog is unpaid relational debt. Write a forgiveness process, clear what you can this week, and practice release-and-repair without denying wisdom (trust rebuild bricks still apply).

### Learning Outcomes
- List your repair backlog

---

## Core Teaching

### 1) Repair backlog

List ruptures still open: with whom, what act, what you owe, what you need, blocked by what (pride, fear, logistics).

### 2) Forgiveness process (design)

1. Name the wrong accurately  
2. Own your part if any  
3. Decide release of revenge debt (Conative)  
4. Communicate if wise and safe  
5. Attach structural next steps (repair / boundaries / trust plan)  
6. Revisit feelings without reversing the decision weekly  

Forgiveness ≠ immediate trust restoration. Trust is bricks (Course 09 W5).

### 3) Release and repair

**Release** = cancel personal vengeance claim.  
**Repair** = restore what can be restored (apology, restitution, changed rail).  
Both may be needed; neither replaces the other.

### 4) One person this week

Choose wisely: not every case is safe for direct contact. Process can begin before conversation; conversation follows wisdom.

### 5) Analogy: bridge repair

You reopen the bridge when structure holds — not when you “feel” the river is friendly. Feelings follow fruit often; do not wait for perfect warmth to begin structure.

### 6) Better whole

Forgiveness is not fusing with the offender’s narrative. You remain a whole person with boundaries.

### 7) Continuity

Cleared backlog makes intimacy possible (Week 7). Bitterness blocks presence even when phones are down.
