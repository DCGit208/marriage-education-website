## The Central Truth

You cannot govern what you cannot name.  
Labeling jars brings order to a pantry; unnamed feelings become anonymous tyrants. Emotional literacy begins with honest vocabulary — for couples and for singles practicing covenant readiness — without baptizing feelings as ontology (“I am my anger”) and without chasing worth through emotional performance (Course 11).

---

## Week Overview

### Core Question
How does naming restore capacity to steward emotion as a teleios person?

### Key Insight
People hide “unacceptable” emotions (anger, envy, fear, desire, contempt, tenderness) and then leak them sideways. Name ten from this week; note which you hide; practice daily naming as Mental clarity + Emotional attribute stewardship — not as dumping, and not as identity.

### Learning Outcomes
By the end of this week you will:

- Distinguish Emotional attribute from Affective faculty in plain speech
- List 10 emotions with precision (beyond mad/sad/fine)
- Identify hidden emotions and their leak routes
- Keep a 7-day emotion log with intensity and cues
- Pass a Naming Emotions Audit without fine-culture fog

## Core Teaching

### 1) Attribute vs faculty (doctrine lock)

| | Meaning in this canon |
|--|------------------------|
| **Emotional attribute** | How feeling states move, intensify, and get governed in the person |
| **Affective faculty** | Capacity to care, value, attach with forethought |

Literacy trains the **attribute** (name and govern feeling). It does not replace Affective care. Collapsing them produces either cold technique or unmanaged care floods.

### 2) Why naming matters (ratiocination)

- Premise 1: Ungoverned emotion still acts (Behavioral leak).
- Premise 2: What is unnamed tends to fuse with identity or hide until ambush.
- Premise 3: Teleios persons remain responsible for speech and acts under feeling (Course 07 / 11).
- Conclusion: Naming is the first stewardship move — epistemic clarity for Conative choice.

### 3) Expand vocabulary (precision reduces explosion)

Beyond mad/sad/fine: frustrated, grieved, envious, ashamed, delighted, tender, lonely, hopeful, contemptuous, peaceful, anxious, jealous, grateful, resentful, eager, overwhelmed, secure, discarded (feeling — not ontology).

Precision is honor. Vague “upset” often hides contempt or fear.

### 4) Hide inventory

Which emotions felt “illegal” in your family, church, gender scripts, or marriage?  
Hiding trains ambush: sarcasm, stonewall, illness theater, pornographic escape, spending, spiritualizing.

Write three hidden emotions + their usual leak route.

### 5) Naming ≠ dumping; naming ≠ fusion

| Healthy naming | Dumping | Fusion |
|----------------|---------|--------|
| “I feel X about Y” | Flood to control | “I am X; fix me” |
| Time-bound | No return | Partner as regulator |
| Invites understanding | Demands absorption | Better-half medicine |

### 6) Emotion log (7 days)

| Time | Emotion word | Intensity 1–5 | Cue | Body | Hidden? | Leak risk |
|------|--------------|---------------|-----|------|---------|-----------|

Morning scan + evening scan minimum.

### 7) Couples and singles tracks

**Couples:** Share one named emotion daily **without fixing** for 3 minutes. Mirror once (Course 10 W4). No advice unless asked.

**Singles:** Name without recruiting a fantasy partner as witness. Journal + one wise friend. Literacy is readiness practice, not a waiting-room identity.

### 8) Analogy: labeled jars

Unlabeled spices ruin the meal. Unlabeled feelings ruin the conversation and the conscience. Labels do not invent the spice; they make cooking possible.

### 9) Course 11 guard

If naming becomes chase (“I must feel the right feelings to be okay”), stop and return to teleios declaration. Feelings report; they do not mint worth.

### 10) Continuity into Week 2

Named emotion still needs a brake. Week 2 installs regulation — active control between feeling and act.

### 11) A starter lexicon (use and add)

Anger family: irritated, frustrated, furious, resentful, bitter, contemptuous.  
Fear family: uneasy, anxious, panicked, dread, insecure.  
Sadness family: down, grieved, lonely, disappointed, hopeless (feeling — not ontology).  
Shame family: embarrassed, humiliated, guilty (act-focused) vs worthless (illegal ontology).  
Gladness family: glad, grateful, delighted, peaceful, hopeful, tender.

For three hidden emotions, write: word · body · cue · what I usually do · design alternative.

### 12) Children / household modeling

If you parent or influence younger people: fine-culture trains them to hide. Naming without dumping models literacy. “I feel frustrated about the mess; I will ask for help” beats silent rage or fog.

### 13) Ratiocination for couples’ nightly share

- Premise 1: Unshared emotion becomes leak or explosion.
- Premise 2: Fixing mid-share trains the speaker to stop naming.
- Premise 3: Mirror listening (Course 10) + time-box protects both.
- Conclusion: Three minutes of named emotion without fixing is literacy practice, not therapy theater.

Script for the listener: paraphrase once; ask “do you want comfort, help, or just witness?”; obey the answer.

### 14) What “good enough for School 1” requires this week

Not poetic feelings essays. A log with precise words, hidden emotions confessed, and at least three shared namings. If you cannot produce the log, you are not ready to claim literacy — return to Week 1 until the jars are labeled.

### 15) Mapping body to word (literacy laboratory)

For five days, when intensity ≥3, complete:

1. Body location (chest, gut, jaw, hands, heat)
2. First imprecise word (“bad / weird / upset”)
3. Precise word from lexicon
4. Need underneath (comfort, justice, rest, boundary, repentance, help)
5. One sentence you could say without dumping

This trains Cognitive precision under Emotional load — the heart of literacy.

### 16) Illegal namings (still name the feeling; fix the ontology)

- “I am anxiety” → “I feel anxious about ______.”
- “I am worthless” → “I feel ashamed about ______ act.”
- “I am my rage” → “I feel furious about ______; I will govern the act.”

Course 11 grammar inside Course 12 vocabulary.

### 17) Couples: the no-fix covenant (write and sign)

> For three minutes after an emotion is named, I will not advise, defend, or problem-solve unless asked. I will mirror once. I will ask comfort / help / witness.

Break the covenant → repair using Course 12 Week 4 early.

### 18) Singles track — loneliness without fusion fantasy

Loneliness is a real emotion word. Illegal move: using fantasy romance as the only witness. Legal move: name lonely; journal; call a real friend; refuse “I am incomplete” ontology. Literacy prepares covenant; it does not postpone honesty until a wedding.

### 19) Depth bar for Week 1 completion

You are not done when you "understand" naming. You are done when the 7-day log exists, three shares happened, hide inventory is written, and you can explain Emotional attribute vs Affective faculty to a witness in under two minutes without notes.

### 20) Teleios + better whole (naming edition)

You are teleios by design. Naming feelings does not invent a self and does not mint worth (Course 11). Feelings report; they do not rule; they do not rewrite ontology.

Better whole: a partner is not a permanent jar-labeler or emotional regulator. Couples share naming; each person still owns their Emotional attribute. Singles refuse fantasy partners as the only witness to interior life.

Illegal: “I am my anger / envy / anxiety.”  
Legal: “I feel ______ about ______; I remain teleios; I will govern the next act.”

### 21) Loop preview

Course 12 loop: **name → regulate → conflict tools → repair.** Week 1 is only the first hinge. Vocabulary without Weeks 2–4 becomes either fine-culture relapse or dumping theater.
