## Core Teaching

### 1) Why repair is the proof

Naming and regulation can be private. Repair is public to the bond. Hebrews-shaped maturity (responsibility under load) shows when torn relatedness is restored by truth.

Matthew 5:23–24: reconciliation priority over religious performance — repair is not optional polish.

### 2) Autopsy template (two attempts)

For each:

- Emotion involved (Week 1 word)  
- What I said/did  
- What I avoided naming  
- What the other needed  
- Self-erasure or fog?  
- Next act kept?  
- Trust at 48 hours (up / flat / down)  

Patterns teach more than intentions.

### 3) Emotional repair ritual

1. Request a repair talk (or scheduled window)  
2. Name the emotion you discharged poorly  
3. Name the act and impact (specific)  
4. Own without fog (“I did…”)  
5. Paraphrase their impact if they share  
6. Ask what would help  
7. One next regulated act + time  
8. Log follow-through 7 days  
9. Do not demand instant “it’s fine” feelings  

### 4) Repair ≠ self-erasure ≠ bribery

| Real repair | Self-erasure | Bribery |
|-------------|--------------|---------|
| Own act | “I’m trash; parent me” | Gift without ownership |
| Next rail | Endless apology fog | Distraction |
| Honor both | Fusion | Avoidance |

Course 11: conviction, not shame ontology, inside the apology.

### 5) Forgiveness vs trust (recall)

Forgiveness may release revenge debt; trust rebuilds by bricks (Course 09–10). Emotional repair can start both — it does not force premature “I trust you.”

### 6) Analogy: mending fabric

Stitched cloth carries weight again. Unmended tears spread under the next load. Pretty patches that skip the stitch (fog apologies) fail at the seam.

### 7) Couples and singles

**Couples:** Practice with spouse; use governance meeting if stuck (Course 09 W6).  
**Singles:** Practice with family/friend you actually ruptured with — literacy is not theoretical.

### 8) Integration — Course 12 loop

Name → Regulate → Conflict tools → Repair.  
Remove repair and the loop is unfinished School 1 work.

### 9) Continuity into Course 13

Temperament differences will tempt contempt (“they’re too much / too cold”). Emotional repair skills + Course 11 ontology prepare you to honor difference without resentment museums.

### 10) Final ratiocination

- Premise 1: Finite persons rupture emotionally.
- Premise 2: Unrepaired emotional rupture compounds into contempt or distance.
- Premise 3: Named, owned, followed-through repair restores capacity without erasing persons.
- Conclusion: Emotional repair is literacy’s capstone — or literacy was theater.

### 11) Repair scripts (adapt; do not recite robotically)

> “I felt ______ and I discharged it by ______. That impacted you by ______. I own that. What would help? My next act is ______ by ______.”

If they are not ready to talk: deliver ownership + next act in writing, invite a time, do not demand absolution.

### 12) When repair is refused

You still own and change. Forgiveness is theirs to give; your Behavioral next act is yours. Do not use their refusal as excuse to return to contempt. Wisdom may add boundaries (Course 09).

### 13) 48-hour trust read (how to score)

After repair: Did they initiate warmth? Did you keep the next act? Did conflict residual stay or clear? Score trust up/flat/down without demanding they perform forgiveness feelings on your timeline.

### 14) Capstone standard for School 1

A School 1 graduate can: name emotion, brake, navigate conflict emotion, and mend. If any leg is missing, the “great school” claim fails in the kitchen. Your charter must name which leg is still weakest and the 30-day rail for it.

### 15) Repair and the four faculties (integration)

A complete emotional repair often needs:

- **Cognitive** — accurate account of what happened
- **Mental** — reject “I am trash” / “they are trash”
- **Emotional** — name what was discharged
- **Affective** — care about impact without fusion
- **Conative** — commit to next act
- **Psychomotor** — show up for the talk; posture; eye contact
- **Behavioral** — keep the next act

Missing faculties produce fog apologies or technical non-human repairs.

### 16) When you are the injured party

Literacy is not only apologizing. If you were harmed:

1. Name your emotion without character assassination
2. State the impact
3. State what would help (boundary, act, time)
4. Refuse moving goalposts as revenge (Course 09)
5. Distinguish forgiveness process from trust bricks

Better whole: you may require structure without becoming a permanent prosecutor.

### 17) Closing charter checklist (Course 12)

Must include: lexicon growth proof, pause card, conflict captain maps, repair ritual, weakest leg + 30-day rail, Course 11 teleios sentences preserved. If chase or feeling-as-ontology returned, confess it in the charter.

### 18) School 1 greatness test

Can your household feel safer because you can mend? If not, literacy is still academic. Mend once more before you collect the certificate in your heart.

### 19) Gift, touch, and timing

Restitution may include a gift or act of service — **after** ownership, never instead of it. Touch may comfort — never coerce forgiveness. Timing: do not force a repair talk at peak red load; schedule it; keep the schedule. Structure is love’s body language.

### 20) Final repair charge

Mend once more than you prefer. Prefer structure over relief. Prefer ownership over fog. Prefer next acts over speeches. Then walk into Course 13 able to honor difference without using emotional illiteracy as an excuse for contempt.
