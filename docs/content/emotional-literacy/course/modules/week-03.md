## The Central Truth

Conflict exposes your emotional formation gaps.  
A storm at sea is navigable with tools — pause, name, repair — not with pretending the ocean is a pond, and not with baptizing blame weather as “honesty.” Literacy that fails in conflict was never literacy; it was classroom vocabulary.

---

## Week Overview

### Core Question
Which emotions run your conflict — and how do you steward them as a whole person?

### Key Insight
Top conflict emotions (anger, fear, shame, contempt, panic, humiliation) drive pattern more than the topic does. Practice pause–name–repair; replace blame with self-stewardship; journal three real conflict windows.

### Learning Outcomes
By the end of this week you will:

- Identify top two conflict emotions with stories and moves
- Install pause–name–repair as a spoken household/personal card
- Replace blame speech with stewardship speech in at least one live conflict
- Keep a 3-day (minimum) conflict emotion journal
- Pass an Emotion in Conflict Audit

## Core Teaching

### 1) Topic vs emotion captain

Most fights have a surface topic (dishes, money, tone, lateness) and an emotion captain (contempt, fear of abandonment, shame, envy). Master the captain or the topic forever returns.

### 3) Pause — name — repair

1. **Pause** — Week 2 brake; set return time if exiting  
2. **Name** — emotion word aloud or on paper (Week 1)  
3. **Own** — your Behavioral move without fog  
4. **Repair** — ask + next act (Course 08 / 10)  

Sequence matters. Naming mid-explosion without pause is still discharge.

### 4) Blame → stewardship

| Blame | Stewardship |
|-------|-------------|
| You made me… | I felt X; I chose Y; I will Z |
| Always / never | This instance |
| Character attack | Act + impact |
| Scorekeeping | One rupture |

Blame protects ego. Stewardship protects design and bond.

### 5) Contempt special warning

Contempt is acid. If contempt is a top emotion, literacy requires naming it early and refusing contempt speech as “just how I talk.” Honor code (Course 09 W2) applies.

### 7) Couples and singles

**Couples:** Use in live conflict with protocol from Course 10.  
**Singles:** Use in family/work/friend conflict — same literacy. Do not wait for marriage to learn navigation.

### 8) Analogy: storm at sea

Denial sinks ships. Panic without instruments sinks ships. Charts, compass, and practiced crew navigate. You are installing instruments.

### 9) Course 11 guard

Conflict shame (“I am a monster for feeling anger”) reinstalls chase. Anger can be named and governed; it is not proof you are non-teleios.

### 10) Continuity into Week 4

Even good navigation leaves tears in the fabric. Week 4 — emotional repair — is the proof of maturity.

### 11) Fear under anger (common captain swap)

Many “anger problems” are fear or shame wearing volume. Literacy asks: after pause, what second emotion appears? Name both. Repair both moves if both drove harm.

### 12) Singles: workplace and family as training ground

Do not postpone conflict literacy until marriage. A spat with a sibling or manager that you pause–name–repair is covenant rehearsal. Journal it with the same honesty.

### 13) Contempt detox micro-drill (3 days)

Each evening list any contempt word, joke, eye-roll, or “always/never” character attack you used. Rewrite each as: emotion named + act named + ask. Contempt is often unprocessed emotion + pride. Literacy refuses the costume.

### 14) When both captains fire

Two people, two emotion captains — e.g., their fear and your contempt. Protocol: sequential naming (one at a time), no dual monologues. First speaker gets mirror; then switch. Without sequence, you get parallel storms.

### 15) Worked example — money fight

Topic: overspend. Captains: your anxiety, their shame, mutual contempt risk.

Sequence: pause → you name anxiety without attacking character → they name shame without collapsing into shame ontology → each owns one Behavioral move → one budget next act → 24h trust read.

If contempt appears (“you’re so irresponsible”), stop topic; repair contempt first. Topic will wait; acid will not.

### 16) Week 3 evidence bar

Produce: two emotion maps, three journal rows, one blame→stewardship rewrite with before/after sentences, and one live conflict where pause–name–repair actually ran. Anything less is incomplete for a school that claims covenant formation.
### 17) The archive problem

Kitchen-sinking (bringing every past wound) is often unprocessed emotion captains wearing history as a weapon. Literacy rule: **one conflict window, one primary emotion captain, one topic.** Archives go to a scheduled repair talk — not to the peak.

### 18) Humor and “standards” as contempt costumes

Write three recent “jokes” or “I’m just saying” lines. For each: Was it play, or contempt with a smile? If contempt, rewrite as named emotion + clean ask. School 1 homes die by a thousand cute cuts.

### 19) Full worked sequence (anger over interrupted evening)

1. Cue: plans changed without consult.
2. Body: heat, jaw.
3. Illegal premise: “They never respect me” (always/never).
4. Pause code; 15-minute return.
5. Name: anger + beneath it, feeling dismissed.
6. Return: own any volume; ask for consult norm going forward.
7. Repair if contempt leaked.
8. Journal row same night.

Practice this sequence once from a real event this week — not a hypothetical.

### 20) Doctrine lock for conflict emotion

Feeling intensely in conflict does not make you immature. **Ungoverned** intensity that harms and won’t repair does. Teleios persons can feel fire and still keep the card.

### 21) After the fight — same-night micro-repair

Even if full repair talk waits until morning, same-night micro-repair is literacy:

> “I was flooding with ______; I owned ______ poorly; I will talk at ______ tomorrow; I am not abandoning.”

This prevents freeze-as-punishment and keeps return-time theology honest.

### 22) Final conflict-emotion charge

Your emotion captain will return. Literacy is not exile of feeling; it is governance under covenant weight. Keep the card on the fridge or phone lock screen. When contempt costumes appear, stop the topic and mend the acid first.

### 23) One-sentence doctrine

Conflict emotion is data under fire — name it, govern it, repair what it harmed, and refuse to crown it as captain of the bond.

### 24) Teleios + better whole (conflict edition)

Feeling fire in conflict does not mean you are non-teleios. Ungoverned harm that will not repair is the maturity failure — not the existence of anger, fear, or shame as feeling reports.

Better whole in the storm: you steward your emotion captain; you do not demand the other person permanently regulate you; you do not erase their agency with fusion (“we’re crazy”) or exile (“you’re the only storm”).

Course 11 guard: conflict shame that says “I am a monster for feeling” reinstalls chase. Name the feeling; own the act; keep the card.

### 25) Loop lock

Pause–name–repair in conflict is the third hinge of **name → regulate → conflict → repair**. Without Week 4 mend, navigation still leaves torn fabric.
