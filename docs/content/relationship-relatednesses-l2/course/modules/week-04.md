# Week 4: Conflict Patterns
## Govern the Weather — Don’t Worship the Storm

> **Week 4 of 6** | 8–10 hours | Pattern Governance  
> **Sources:** Course 08 communication/repair · Week 3 power · James 1:19

---

## The Central Truth

Conflict is a **pattern to be governed**, not a storm to be feared — and not a sport to be won.  
Weather maps predict fronts. Your conflict has fronts too: cue → move → counter-move → rupture or repair.

---

## Week Overview

### Core Question
How is conflict governed as pattern, not feared as fate?

### Key Insight
Destructive conflict feels unique every time; it is usually the same two patterns wearing new costumes. Name them, replace one, practice pause–ask–listen under load.

### Learning Outcomes
- Identify top two conflict patterns  
- Replace one destructive pattern with a design pattern  
- Practice pause, ask, listen in live conflict  
- Keep a one-week conflict pattern journal  
- Pass a Conflict Patterns Audit  

---

## Core Teaching

### 1) Conflict is not failure

Finite persons differ. Conflict can clarify design. **Ungoverned** conflict damages honor, trust, and body.

### 2) Common pattern pairs

| Pattern | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Pursue–withdraw | One presses; one exits |
| Attack–defend | Contempt vs justification |
| Escalate–escalate | Volume and kitchen-sink |
| Peace-fake–explode | Soft lie then detonation |
| Scorekeep–scorekeep | Archive warfare |

Pick your top two honestly.

### 3) Design replacement

Example: pursue–withdraw →  

> At cue (raised voice), we pause 20 minutes; withdrawer sets return time; pursuer journals one ask; resume with paraphrase first.

Write one replacement card for your top pattern.

### 4) Pause — ask — listen

1. **Pause** — Behavioral interrupt (Course 07)  
2. **Ask** — one clarifying question, not a trap  
3. **Listen** — paraphrase before rebuttal (Course 08 W3)  

### 5) Journal fields (one week)

| Date | Cue | My pattern | Their pattern | Pause? | Outcome | Repair? |
|------|-----|------------|---------------|--------|---------|---------|

### 6) Analogy: weather map

You cannot outlaw weather. You can refuse to picnic in lightning. Pattern literacy is relational meteorology.

### 7) Power in conflict

Week 3: the one who can leave, shout louder, or withhold may “win” and still lose the bond. Govern power mid-conflict: no coercion, no silent abandonment without return time.

### 8) Better whole

“Conflict means we’re wrong for each other” can be fear.  
“Conflict means I get to fuse or flee” can be immaturity.  
Governed conflict is adult relatedness.

### 9) Continuity

Broken patterns bruise trust. Week 5 rebuilds with bricks, not speeches.
