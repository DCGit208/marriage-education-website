## The Central Truth

Conflict often reveals **temperament differences**, not character defects.  
Musical notes clash until arranged; curiosity arranges better than judgment. Defect theology (“they’re broken for being slow/fast/intense/quiet”) is contempt with a moral costume. Endless analysis without decision is also failure — curiosity is a tool, not a hiding place.

## Core Teaching

### 1) Two layers of a fight

1. **Topic** — money, chores, sex timing, in-laws, punctuality  
2. **Flavor clash** — fast closer vs slow processor; engagers vs withdrawers; high intensity vs low  

If you only fight the topic, the clash returns dressed as a new topic.

### 3) Pause and ask (temperament edition)

Before reacting:

1. Brake (Course 12 / 07)  
2. Ask internally: “Defect — or different pace/intensity?”  
3. Ask aloud one curious question  
4. Then own your move and use conflict card  

Curiosity that never reaches a decision is avoidance. Time-box: curiosity → decision window.

### 4) Curiosity scripts (examples — write yours)

- “Help me understand what you need when you’re quiet.”  
- “I move fast; do you need twenty minutes before we decide?”  
- “My intensity is up; is volume the problem or the content?”  
- “I’m not withdrawing to punish — I need a return time. I’ll be back at ____.”  

### 5) Judgment → curiosity → still responsibility

| Judgment | Curiosity | Responsibility |
|----------|-----------|----------------|
| You’re cold | What’s your processing need? | I will not stonewall without return |
| You’re controlling | What’s your pace fear? | I will not steamroll deadlines of care |
| You’re dramatic | What’s the intensity signal? | I will not use volume as dominance |

### 7) Analogy: musical notes

Dissonance can resolve to harmony. Contempt is refusing to read the score. Walking off stage mid-piece without return time is not “temperament” — it is ungoverned Behavioral exit.

### 8) Power and temperament (Course 09 W3)

The faster / louder / more socially skilled person can “win” conflicts while losing the bond. Submit power to protocol. The slower processor needs voice without being labeled defective.

### 9) Course 11–12 guards

- Do not shame yourself for having a flavor (“I am broken for being intense”).  
- Do not dump unregulated emotion and call it “my fiery temperament.”  
- Name emotion (Course 12) *and* read temperament (this course).

### 10) Continuity into Week 3

### 11) Pursue–withdraw with temperament glasses

Often pursue = higher intensity / faster pace; withdraw = reserved / slow processor. Protocol: withdrawer sets return time; pursuer journals one ask; both refuse defect labels (“needy” / “cold”) during the pause.

### 12) Sexual timing conflicts (marriage)

Pace and intensity differences often surface sexually. Honor means: no coercion, no contempt for desire level differences, clean asks, Course 06 W11 loyalty. Difference is not defect; pressure is not intimacy (Course 10 W7).

### 13) Decision time-box template

Curiosity window: 15 minutes.  
Then: one decision, or one scheduled decision time within 24 hours.  
Write the decision.  
If slow processor needs more time: agree the deadline now — not infinite delay.

This keeps Week 2 from becoming endless soft conflict.

### 14) In-law / extended family flavor clashes

Temperament contempt often enters through family of origin stories (“in our family we just say it”). Translate: different intensity norms. Honor your spouse’s flavor in front of your family. Public contempt of your spouse’s temperament is covenant betrayal with manners.

Vague maps produce vague marriages.

### 16) The “spiritualized preference” problem

Sometimes we baptize our temperament as the godly one (“gentle always waits”; “leaders always push”). Test preferences against design fruit: truth, fidelity, honor, repair. Preferring your own flavor is normal; sacralizing it is pride.

### 17) Practice drill — 72-hour curiosity

In the next 72 hours, during one real friction:

1. Pause
2. Ask one curious question from your card
3. Time-box a decision
4. Own your flavor move
5. Journal the row

If you avoid all friction to protect the assignment, you fail the assignment.

### 18) Doctrine lock

Difference is not defect. Difference is also not destiny. Teleios persons with different temperaments still keep covenants, tell truth, and repair. Curiosity serves responsibility — it does not replace it.

### 19) Extended case — bedtime clash

Fast closer wants lights out decision now; slow processor needs twenty minutes. Contempt scripts: “You’re a child” / “You’re a dictator.”  
Design play: curious ask + time-box (“twenty minutes, then we decide”) + no volume dominance + morning review if it failed.  
Journal it once this week even if bedtime is not your issue — use your real analogue (leaving parties, ending visits, closing purchases).

### 20) What “mastery” means here

Not never clashing. Mastery is: shorter contempt half-life, faster curiosity, kept time-boxes, repaired defect-labels same day. Measure that — not vibes.

### 21) Final conflict charge

Curiosity without decision breeds fog. Decision without curiosity breeds tyranny. Hold both. Time-box. Repair defect labels same day. That is temperament intelligence under fire — worthy of a great school.

### 22) One-sentence doctrine

In conflict, curiosity opens the door and responsibility walks through it — on time.

### 23) School 1 greatness evidence bar

By week’s end you must show: two conflict maps with teleios lines intact, one curiosity question asked live, one time-boxed decision kept, and one same-day repair of a defect-label. Without that evidence, temperament talk remains theory — not School 1 fruit.
