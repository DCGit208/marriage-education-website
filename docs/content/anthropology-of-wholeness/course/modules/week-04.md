If Week 4 feels repetitive, that is the point. Formation returns consciousness to design. Repetition does not manufacture teleios; it keeps education and accountability in view.

### 2) The Personal Anthropology Charter

Your charter is not poetry. It is a covenant-facing document with five sections:

1. **Design** — What a human is (your Week 1 definition, refined)  
2. **Image** — What you receive and will not redefine  
3. **Holiness** — The state you do not manufacture: “be holy, for I am holy”  
4. **Likeness** — How formed character aligns life to image and holiness  
5. **Personal Development Map** — Attributes, skills, capacities, and the responsibilities proper to each  
6. **Covenant rule** — *I refuse “better half.” I am teleios; my spouse is a better whole. I will not use marriage to manufacture what design already gave.*

Optional seventh line for married learners: *How I will live responsibly with my spouse this month without making them my ontology or formation program.*

Read the charter aloud weekly for 30 days.

### 3) Ordinary life is the disclosure

Theory passes in quiet mornings. Anthropology is tested when:

- you are tired  
- you are criticized  
- desire pulls  
- someone fails you  
- you fail someone  
- money is tight  
- plans break  
- silence would be easier than truth  

Hebrews 5:14 describes those who **are of full age** and whose senses, in the course of living, are exercised to discern good and evil. Use does not create maturity any more than driving creates a car. Living discloses whether the mature person is mindful, educated, accountable, and acting according to design.

### 4) Repair as anthropology

Anthropology that is not espoused hides, blames, or performs. A teleios person living responsibly **repairs**:

1. Name impact (not only intention)  
2. Own without excuse  
3. State the structural change  
4. Ask what rebuild looks like  
5. Keep the commitment for 7 days minimum  

Repair script (adapt, do not theatricalize):

> “Here’s the impact I caused: ____.  
> I own it without excuse.  
> The structural change I’m making is: ____.  
> What would rebuild look like for you this week?”

Repair does not make you teleios. It is fruit of responsibility and likeness. Persistent refusal to repair is evidence that you are not living according to the covenant responsibility you claim.

## Case Contrast

### Charter on paper, chaos in life
Beautiful anthropology language. Same blame patterns. Same unfinished commitments. The course became content consumption. Friends are impressed; the household is not safer.

### Charter in life
Fewer slogans. Greater responsibility. Attribute education continues after “graduation.” Marriage (or dating) stops being the manufacturer of personhood. The home feels the difference before the brand does.

### The almost-ready person
High mental clarity, improving behavior, still recruits romance for emotional regulation. Week 4 names it: the learner says “teleios” but still lives the “better half” bargain. Extend education and accountability. That is strength, not failure.

## Daily Responsibilities (7-day Ordinary-Life Audit)

Run **all** of these every day (35–45 min total).

### Morning (12 min) — Charter consciousness
Read your draft charter. Circle one line you are responsible to espouse today. Speak the closing declaration.

### Midday (5 min) — Attribute responsibility
One accountable action from the Week 3 attribute you selected for greater mindfulness.

### Evening (15–20 min) — Integration log
Record:

1. Design: Did I espouse teleios, or did I live as if incomplete?  
2. Image/holiness/likeness: Which did I understand clearly, and where did I collapse them?  
3. Attributes: Which did I live responsibly, and which did I refuse?  
4. Repair: What must be repaired by tomorrow morning?  
5. Better whole: Did “complete me / better half” expectations appear?

### Day 7 — Finalize charter + 30-day plan
Lock the one-page charter. Write the maintenance plan (below). Share charter with one witness if possible.
